# Meal plan — week of Tuesday 10-11-2026 ✴️

*Built around a vegetable box delivered Tuesday 10-11-2026. Six days, Tuesday to Sunday, lunch and dinner. The plan is **component-first**: one prep session on Tuesday converts the perishable bulk of the box into four stable components, and every meal after that is an assembly job rather than a cook from scratch.*

> ⚠ **PLANNED, NOT COOKED.** No quantity here has been weighed, and the mushroom roasting loss it leans on is an estimate with no measurement behind it (see **The one number this plan is guessing**).

> ⚠ **The vendor's "Chinese Spinach (Puay Leng)" is two different plants under one label.** Puay leng (菠菱) is *true* spinach, *Spinacia oleracea*; Chinese spinach is bayam / 苋菜, *Amaranthus*. The project has them as a recorded name collision. It matters only in that the stems behave differently — Holger puts puay leng stems in the shakshuka cut small and they crunch, which is turgor and holds only if they are not submerged for long. Either plant works for the shakshuka; check which one actually arrives.

**Three facts set the whole plan.**

1. **It is a mushroom box.** 2300 g of mushroom, 2.3× the next-biggest item. Seven of the twelve meals have mushroom in them and no amount of cleverness changes that — the variation has to come from **form** (coins, pieces, purée, hotpot, a baked layer) and from cuisine, not from pretending the mushrooms are not there.
2. **There is almost no protein in it.** A dozen eggs, all of them committed to the shakshuka. Every dinner below names its protein from the pantry — ✅ tau kwa, ✅ tamago tofu, ✅ frozen edamame, ✅ soya milk, ✅ barley — and those are the things to check before Tuesday, not the vegetables.
3. **The perishability spread is six days to three months.** Greens, coriander and the two soft mushrooms have to be used or cooked in the first 48 hours; onion, carrot, beetroot, butternut and purple sweet potato will still be fine next weekend. So the plan front-loads mushroom and greens and puts root and squash at the weekend, which is also the right way round for effort: the big bake lands on Saturday.

## Tuesday prep session — ~90 minutes, mostly oven time

This is the part that makes the rest of the week quick. Four components, all of them keeping 4–5 days in the fridge.

| # | Component | From | Yields |
|:-:|:----------|:-----|:-------|
| **P1** | 🍄 **Roasted mushroom** — hard and dry, bare preheated sheet, oil only once the surfaces have gone matte. 3–4 trays, not crowded. | 🍄‍🟫 shiitake **caps** 750 g + 🟤 portobello 1000 g | **~875 g** |
| **P2** | 🍄 **Mushroom purée** — 400 g of P1 blitzed coarse with a splash of the pan liquor. **This is the Lara-safe form.** | from P1 | 400 g |
| **P3** | 🎃 **Roasted butternut + purple sweet potato** — 20 mm dice, ~1 g oil per 100 g, 220 °C convection. ~16% loss, measured on file. | 🎃 500 g + 🍠 500 g | **~840 g** |
| **P4** | 🌾 **Thai aromatic base** — tender lemongrass, makrut leaves, 🌱 coriander stems and roots, ginger, pounded or blitzed. Freeze in two portions. | 🌾 100 g + 🍃 15 g + stems | 2 × ~60 g |

**Alongside, costing nothing:**

- 🍄‍🟫 **Shiitake stems straight to the freezer** in a labelled bag — about 150 g. They are the dashi ingredient, and frozen is where they belong: the cell walls rupture, and they go into an *already hot* bath so the enzyme that destroys the GMP never gets its window. See `../docs/TECHNIQUES.md`.
- 🍃 **Makrut leaves flat in a freezer bag, 🌾 lemongrass stalks whole beside them.** 200 g of makrut leaves is months of supply and they freeze without losing anything. Do not try to use them up this week.
- 🫐 **Beetroot roasts Wednesday, not Tuesday** — whole, in foil, 200 °C, 50–60 minutes, oven already on for something else. It keeps a week cooked and it is the one item that is genuinely better prepped ahead.

### The one number this plan is guessing

⚠⚠ **P1 assumes a ~50% loss on hard-roasted mushroom, and there is no roasted-mushroom row in `../docs/TECHNIQUES.md` at all.** Every figure downstream of P1 moves if that is wrong, and it is the third session in a row where this measurement was available and skipped — the three-mushroom soup missed it, and the Zwiebelkuchen missed it twice, which is why that bake's 45% loss cannot be attributed to anything (observation 59 in `../docs/RATINGS.md`).

**So weigh it: 1750 g of trimmed raw mushroom in, and the total off the trays once cooled.** One number, and it closes a gap that has cost the project two bad yield forecasts. Weighing the shiitake and the portobello separately is better still — they are different water contents and they are being treated as interchangeable here on no evidence.

## 🌰 Buckwheat in place of rice

Replaces ✅ black rice in all four meals that had it: Tuesday lunch, Tuesday dinner, Thursday dinner and Saturday lunch. **~1.2 kg dry across the week** — 90 g for the solo lunch, 400 g for each family dinner, 300 g for the cold Saturday slaw.

**Quantities and method, from the one measurement on file.** Kasha pressure-cooked by absorption at **1.75 : 1 water to grain by weight**, 0 minutes at low pressure with a 10-minute defined release, yields **2.53×** (250 g → 633 g, 2026-10-20). So 400 g dry gives about **1010 g cooked** — a family bowl base. See `../docs/TECHNIQUES.md`. ⚠ That row forecasts *this method only*; a water-generous or drained stovetop pan takes up far more, and USDA's own dry/cooked pair implies the grain would keep absorbing to 3.76× if the water were there.

**Three things change, and only one of them is a problem.**

1. ⚠ **It collapses under a wet sauce where black rice held.** Buckwheat has no gluten and a soft starchy endosperm, and it keeps absorbing and softening after it is cooked. The chew of black rice was doing real work in the Tuesday bowls and under the Thursday curry. Two fixes, both cheap: cook those two **drier — 1.5 : 1 rather than 1.75 : 1** — and **serve the Thursday curry alongside rather than ladled over**, which is why that line now reads "alongside". Spreading the cooked grain out to cool lets the surface steam escape instead of soaking back in.
2. ✅ **It is better than black rice for the Saturday slaw.** Cold buckwheat holds its shape and its texture; cold black rice goes hard. The project already has a cold buckwheat salad recipe, so this is the proven use rather than the risky one.
3. **It cooks in a fraction of the time** — 0 minutes plus a release, against 35–45 minutes for black rice. That helps Tuesday, which is the busiest day in the plan.

**Kasha or raw groats, and it matters.** ❓ The pantry lists **kasha** (pre-toasted, dark, strongly earthy) as unconfirmed, and the measured row above is kasha, so that is what these quantities assume. **If what gets bought is raw green groats, toast them dry in a pan first** — that is the difference between separate grains and porridge, and it is not optional.

**Buckwheat has never drawn a complaint in this family.** The Cold Buckwheat, Asparagus & Black-Eyed Bean Salad scored Lara 7.2 and Jannes 6.9 → 8.0, but both of those were about the rocket and the cold blanched asparagus, not the grain. ⚠ The one thing to watch is **Jannes and kasha's toasted earthiness**, which is more assertive than black rice — a watch, not a flag.

**It is not a cereal.** Buckwheat is a pseudocereal in the same family as rhubarb and sorrel, and the practical consequence is that it carries lysine, which rice does not. It complements the week's edamame and barley instead of duplicating them, and it brings more protein and magnesium than black rice. The loss is the anthocyanin colour, which was doing visual work in the Tuesday bowls.
