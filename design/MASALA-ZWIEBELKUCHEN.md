# Masala-Zwiebelkuchen — design + test-run sheet

A German/South-Asian savoury bake for the **Christmas event at the German school**. Sheet-pan Zwiebelkuchen with the Speck replaced by browned tau kwa and king oyster mushroom, and the Kümmel replaced by Kreuzkümmel.

> **Status: designed, not yet cooked.** Quantities below are the *plan* for the first test run. Nothing in this file is measured. Every number marked **→ record** is a weigh-in the test run must produce before a recipe file or a `.foodnoms` file can be written.

---

## The idea in one line

**Zwiebelkuchen is seasoned with Kümmel. This one is seasoned with Kreuzkümmel.**

Caraway out, cumin in. The two words differ by one prefix in German, the two seeds are both Apiaceae, and both occupy the same pungent-seed register in a dish. That single swap is the whole fusion argument and it has to be *visible* — whole cumin scattered on the surface before baking, exactly where the caraway would have gone.

Everything else follows from it: if the top seed is cumin, the onion layer can carry a tadka, and the dish stays recognisably Zwiebelkuchen rather than becoming an onion tart with curry powder in it.

---

## Why this form and not another

Three candidates were considered for the school contribution.

| Candidate | Fusion legibility | Survives a buffet | Risk |
|:----------|:------------------|:------------------|:-----|
| **Masala-Zwiebelkuchen** | Good — the Kümmel joke lands with Germans | **Yes** — squares, eaten by hand, better at room temperature than hot | Low |
| Laugen-Kipferl with potato-pea filling | Best — a samosa in pretzel dough | **No** — a lye crust goes leathery in Singapore humidity within the hour | Medium (lye sourcing) |
| Savoury Stollen (tau kwa log for the marzipan rope) | Strong, maximum Christmas | Poor — needs a knife and a plate | High |

Zwiebelkuchen wins on the one criterion that matters most at a school buffet: **nothing about it depends on a crust staying crisp.** Anything baked crisp in the morning is soft by the time it is eaten here.

---

## Hard constraints

- **No chilli at all.** Spice as aroma, not as heat. There will be a room full of German children. This is not the usual Jannes accommodation — it is the whole audience.
- **Vegetarian throughout.** The Speck is gone by default, not as a substitution problem to solve later.
- **Must be eaten standing up, with fingers, off a napkin.** Squares, firm base, no sauce.
- **Must hold four hours at ambient Singapore temperature** between leaving the oven and being eaten.

---

## The Speck substitution needs two components, not one

Speck contributes four things: discrete chewy pieces, fat, salt, and smoke. Tofu covers the first only, and tau kwa cubes left pale in a custard are the standard failure of this substitution — they read as blank white squares.

| Function of the Speck | Replacement |
|:----------------------|:------------|
| Discrete pieces | 🧊 Tau kwa, 8 mm dice, **browned hard and dry** |
| Chew | 🍄 King oyster, 8 mm dice, **browned hard and dry** |
| Fat | 🥑 Oil in the tadka and in the browning |
| Salt | Budgeted explicitly — see below |
| Smoke | **Dropped.** ❓ Smoked paprika carries the standing Lara flag and a masala version does not need it. |

**Use tau kwa and king oyster in roughly equal parts.** Tofu alone gives protein without texture, mushroom alone gives texture without protein.

---

## Mushrooms in a custard are a water problem

Recorded loss for wok-fried mushrooms in this kitchen is **30%**. Any of that water still in the mushroom at assembly comes back out during the bake and sits under the custard.

Use the **two-phase method** established 01-11-2026 for king oyster:

1. **Oven, 220 °C convection with fan, no oil**, until the cut faces go from wet to matte.
2. **Skillet, 3–4 minutes**, with the oil, to brown.

Phase 1 removes the water so that phase 2 has nothing to release onto the pan. A flat skillet beats the wok here — the wok's curved base only puts a few pieces in contact at once.

> **This test run produces the first oven-roasted king oyster losses figures.** Weigh raw, after phase 1, and after phase 2. No row on file separates oven-dewatering from pan-browning.

### Base

| Ingredient | Amount |
|:-----------|-------:|
| 🫓 Atta (chakki-ground wholemeal) | **300 g** — 🛒 not recorded locally |
| 💪 Vital wheat gluten | **15 g** — 🛒 absent, see below |
| 💧 Water, warm | ~215 ml — **by feel, not by number** |
| 🫧 Instant yeast | 7 g |
| 🧂 Salt | 4 g |
| 🥑 Neutral oil | 30 g |

**No white flour anywhere in this bake** — not in the base, and not in the custard, which carried 25 g of it until 2026-11-03. If the base comes out crumbly the answer is still not white flour: see the zero-white-flour section below for the ladder.

Thin, pressed out to the tray with a **2 cm rim**.

> **Autolyse: mix the 🫓 and 💧 alone and rest 30 minutes before adding 🫧, 🧂 and 🥑, then knead.** The single highest-value step in this dough and it costs nothing but clock time. The bran hydrates and softens so its edges cut less, and gluten develops without work. Do it whatever the flour turns out to be.

> **Pre-bake the base 12 minutes at 220 °C fan before topping.** Not traditional. A thin yeast base under a wet topping goes soggy underneath, and a square that cannot be picked up by hand is useless at a buffet. **12 rather than 10 because the base is wholemeal** — see below.

#### Mustard seed: either colour

**Brown or yellow both work, and the difference mostly does not survive the pan.** Brown mustard's sharpness is allyl isothiocyanate, which is volatile and heat-labile — frying destroys most of it, which is why popped mustard reads nutty and faintly bitter rather than hot. Yellow's pungent compound is non-volatile and milder to start with, and also largely goes. What is left in both cases is the roasted seed. Yellow seeds are larger, so they are more visible and pop more vigorously.

What yellow costs is some of the characteristic tadka aroma. What it buys is a small nudge back toward the German side — **yellow is the European mustard** (Senf, German pickles, Zwiebelkuchen's own region) and brown is the Indian one — which is worth a little now that the base is atta rather than German wholemeal. ⚠ **Do not overweight that:** at 3 g in a ~1870 g bake nobody will identify the variety.

**Use whichever is in the cupboard. Do not make a trip for brown.**

#### The brief is maximum atta with no crumble, and the lever is not the blend ratio

Settled 2026-11-03 after going atta → ordinary wholemeal → atta. **Holger's constraint: not crumbly, and — confirmed 2026-11-03 — no white flour at all.** Atta satisfies it better than ordinary wholemeal, because chakki grinding is much finer than roller milling and the wheat is harder, so **less gluten is cut per gram of bran** and the wholemeal fraction can go higher for the same structural integrity.

**But the blend ratio is the wrong lever.** Two things fix crumbliness without a gram of white flour:

1. **💪 Vital wheat gluten, 15 g into 300 g of atta (5%).** Puts back exactly what the bran dilutes. This is the standard technique for high-wholemeal bread and it is what makes **100% atta** possible here.
2. **Autolyse, 30 minutes.** See above. Free.

⚠ **Why atta needs the help at all: it is a flour for *unleavened* flatbread.** The tell is that Indian leavened breads — naan, kulcha — use maida, refined white, not atta. Bran particles interrupt the gluten matrix, and **crumbliness is exactly how test question 1 fails**: a square that falls apart in the hand is useless at a buffet, and the 2 cm rim has to hold the custard while it sets.

⚠ **Second risk, less obvious: a wholemeal base is more porous, so moisture from the topping migrates through it faster.** It may go soggy *sooner* than a white base, not later. Hence 12 minutes of pre-bake rather than 10. This holds for any wholemeal and is not specific to atta.

⚠ **Hydration goes up a long way.** Atta's chakki-damaged starch is unusually thirsty — 10–15 percentage points above white, more than ordinary wholemeal's 5–10 — and the gluten flour drinks as well. **~215 ml at 100% atta**, against the 180 ml this plan first specified for white. Judge by feel.

⚠ **Do NOT raise the oil to fight crumbliness.** Fat shortens a dough, so more oil makes it *more* crumbly, not less. The 30 g stays.

✅ **What the base gains by being atta:** this is the only structural fusion element in the dish. Everything else South Asian here is a seasoning — cumin, amchur, shiitake, ginger, turmeric, mustard — while the flour is a third of the bake by mass.

**→ Test both halves on one tray: 100% atta + 15 g gluten against 100% atta plain.** Same topping across both, one bake.

This is *not* the comparison first written down. The original was 100% atta + gluten against the 80/20 atta/white fallback, because the gluten flour might not arrive. **It was ordered on 2026-11-03, so that insurance is dead and the fallback half would be testing a path Holger does not want anyway.** The brief is zero white flour, so the question worth one tray is **does the gluten earn its place** — and both halves stay white-free whatever the answer. **If the plain half holds together, the base gets simpler permanently and the gluten goes back to being seitan flour.**

#### Zero white flour is a rule, not a preference

Confirmed 2026-11-03. **The 80/20 atta/white fallback is struck from the plan**, not kept as insurance — a fallback nobody intends to use is just a tempting escape from solving the actual problem.

⚠ **It also catches the custard**, which carried 25 g of plain white flour until this rule landed. See the Custard section — it is now 15 g of corn flour.

**If both base halves crumble, the escalation ladder stays white-free.** In order:

1. **More gluten** — 15 g → 25 g, i.e. 8% of the flour rather than 5%. The first and most likely fix.
2. **Less oil** — 30 g → 20 g. Fat shortens a dough, so cutting it helps for the same reason adding it would hurt.
3. **A thicker base.** A thin pressed base is structurally the weakest form available. Thickening it is free and costs only the squares-per-tray count.
4. **An egg in the dough.** Coagulated egg protein binds. Changes the character towards an enriched dough, so it is last.

Sifting the bran out is **not** on the ladder — that is manufacturing white flour by hand and defeats the point.

### Onion layer

| Ingredient | Amount |
|:-----------|-------:|
| 🧅 Onions, peeled and sliced | **1200 g** |
| 🌰 Cumin seed, whole (tadka) | 4 g |
| 🌼 Mustard seed, brown **or yellow** | 3 g |
| 🌿 Fennel seed | 2 g |
| 🫚 Fresh ginger, grated | 15 g |
| 🌻 Ground turmeric | 3 g |
| 🥑 Neutral oil (tadka) | 15 g |
| 🧀 Nutritional yeast | 5 g |
| 🍄‍🟫 Dried shiitake powder | 4 g — ❓ confirm stock |
| 🥭 Amchur (dried green mango powder) | 5 g — in late, 🛒 on order |
| 🧂 Salt | 1 g |

**1200 g looks wrong and is correct.** The onion volume is the body of the dish — a thin onion layer is the most common way this bake fails.

> **Sweat the onions soft with no colour.** Zwiebelkuchen onion is pale and juicy, not caramelised. Browning them collapses the volume to a fraction and buries the spices underneath the sweetness — the opposite of what was wanted in the 26-10 butter masala, where caramelising was the right call.

Order: bloom 🌰🌼🌿 in the 🥑, then 🫚, then 🌻, then the 🧅 go in on top of that. 🧀 and 🍄‍🟫 go in with the onions, so both have liquid and time to hydrate. **No garlic** — Zwiebelkuchen has none, and onion is the point of the dish.

#### The missing acid, and why it ends up as amchur in the onions

⚠⚠ **Defect in the first version of this plan, found 2026-11-02 while answering a question about chestnuts.** Zwiebelkuchen's binder is sour cream or crème fraîche and **the tang is part of what the dish is**. The custard here drops it, because souring the soya milk risks curdling it in the oven — and the first draft then failed to put the acid anywhere else. **1200 g of sweated onion with no acid against it** is the sweetest thing in the bake with nothing cutting it.

⚠ Whatever the fix, it is *not* a reason to sour the custard after all. The `PANTRY.md` note on NutriSoy is explicit: the unnamed stabiliser helps against acid curdling but **does not repeal the rule** — soy protein still flocculates near pH 4.5.

**The acid went through three forms in one day. The settled answer is 🥭 amchur, 5 g, into the onions late.**

| Candidate | Verdict |
|:----------|:--------|
| 🍋 Lemon juice / cider vinegar, 15 g in the onions | First fix. Works, but it is a liquid going into a bake whose governing constraint is keeping water out. **Kept as the fallback.** |
| 🍒 Sumac, 6 g on top | Holger's suggestion. Better form (a powder) and a good spike, but **Levantine — a third cuisine in a two-cuisine dish.** Superseded. |
| 🥭 **Amchur, 5 g in the onions** | **Adopted.** A powder like the sumac, and **on-register**: chaat masala, chana masala, stuffed parathas. Removes the identity objection instead of tolerating it. |

**Why the placement changed with the ingredient.** Sumac was going on top because it is a finishing spice — its malic acid survives the oven but its fruity top note does not — and because a surface scatter delivers acid as a spike rather than evenly, which `../docs/TECHNIQUES.md` records as the better pattern against a sweet base (*a concentrated salt spike, so each mouthful varies instead of the whole pot being salted up*). **Neither argument carries over.** Amchur is conventionally a cooked-in spice, and it is **pale beige, so a surface dusting would be invisible** on a pale onion tart. There is no point scattering something nobody can see. The top stays 🌰 cumin only, which is the thing that has to be seen.

**Dose and handling.** 5 g, not the sumac's 6 g — amchur is potent and ordinary home use is around a teaspoon for a whole dish, so 5 g on a tray is already generous. **In late**, at the end of the sweat: it is a dried fruit powder, and long dry heat dulls it and can push it towards bitter.

⚠ **If the amchur has not arrived when the test run happens, do not wait for it.** None of the five questions the test has to answer depends on which acid is in the dish — base firmness, custard weeping, Zwiebelkuchen identity, tray yield and the flour comparison are all independent of it. Run the test on the lemon-juice fallback and swap the amchur in for the event itself.

#### Why shiitake is a powder here and not a third solid

🧀 supplies **glutamate** only. 🍄‍🟫 supplies **guanylate**, and the two multiply rather than add (`../docs/TECHNIQUES.md` → umami). Guanylate is heat-stable, so a 40-minute bake costs nothing, and the powder carries no water and no salt. **That is why the nooch drops from 8 g to 5 g rather than staying at 8 and having shiitake added on top** — with the pair present, less total does more.

⚠ **Rehydrated shiitake must not go in as a solid.** It is wet, which reintroduces exactly the problem the two-phase mushroom method exists to remove, and it neither browns nor holds a chew, so it cannot do the job the 🍄 king oyster is doing.

⚠ **Keep the dose small, and this is a judgement about the dish, not about the ingredient.** 1200 g of sweated onion plus browned tofu plus browned king oyster plus nooch is already a lot of savoury depth. The failure mode is that the bake stops reading as Zwiebelkuchen and becomes generically savoury, which breaks the one thing the design rests on. 🍄‍🟫 is also brown and the onion layer is meant to be pale — 4 g in 1200 g will not shift the colour, 10 g would.

⚠ **The ✅ Kombu Shiitake Dashi powder is the wrong substitute.** It is 4.8% shiitake, so 10 g of it delivers 0.48 g of actual mushroom, and at 11.4 g salt per 100 g it would put 1.14 g into a 5.5 g budget. That is a dosing constraint on a dish for the kids' table, not an objection to the salt. Grind whole dried caps instead — they are brittle and a spice grinder or processor handles them.

**Optional, free:** the base needs ~215 ml of 💧 anyway. Soak dried caps in it, use the liquid in the dough, and the mushroom flavour costs no moisture anywhere it matters. Low impact, since the base is thin and structural.

### Custard

| Ingredient | Amount |
|:-----------|-------:|
| 🥚 Egg | 3 |
| 🥛 Soya milk (F&N NutriSoy) | 200 ml |
| 🌽 Corn flour (cornstarch) | 15 g — ✅ in stock, **slake it first** |

> ⚠ **No acid in the custard.** The traditional binder is sour cream or crème fraîche, and the tang is the point of it. Soy protein flocculates near pH 4.5, so souring the soya milk in a custard that then goes into a hot oven is a curdling risk. Either leave it unsoured, or use real cream — which is an unresolved pantry item (`PANTRY.md` flags the milk default as "don't silently resolve, ask") and needs its panel recorded first.

> ⚠⚠ **The zero-white-flour rule catches the custard too.** This was 25 g of plain white flour until 2026-11-03 and it is easy to miss, because the base is where everyone looks. **Corn flour replaces it** — maize, not wheat, so the rule is satisfied, and it was already in stock, which besan was not.
>
> ✅ **15 g, not 25 g.** Corn flour is near-pure starch and a much stronger thickener than wheat or gram flour, so it is not a gram-for-gram swap. It also **sets firmer and more sliceable**, which suits a tart that has to hold a square in the hand.
>
> ⚠ **Slake it.** Mix the 15 g with a little of the cold 🥛 to a smooth paste before the rest of the milk and the 🥚 go in. Added dry it will lump.
>
> ⚠ **Check which "corn flour" is in the cupboard.** In the UK and Singapore it means **cornstarch** — fine, white, pure starch. In the US the same words mean finely milled maize, which is yellow, gritty and thickens quite differently. This project already has a case of one English name covering two unrelated things: the 2026-10-09 trip that came back with 决明子 cassia *seed* instead of cassia bark (`../docs/PANTRY.md:179`). White and powdery is the right one.
>
> **Besan was the first choice and is the better one on paper** — on-register for the South Asian half, and it would have added about 5.5 g of protein that corn flour does not. Dropped because it is not in stock and corn flour is. Trivial against a ~1870 g bake, but it is a real difference rather than a free swap. It is now on the list as 🛒 in `../docs/PANTRY.md` → Pulses and grains, with its USDA figures and the chana-dal-versus-kabuli distinction, so a later version of this bake can use it.

### Bake

**200 °C convection with fan, 35–40 minutes.** The Fujioh FV-EL61 has eight modes and "Convection with fan" is the one wanted — top and bottom elements plus the fan, 50–250 °C, default 220 °C. A fan oven runs effectively hotter than a conventional one, so this sits below the 200 °C a conventional Zwiebelkuchen recipe would specify.

## Salt budget

Target band for this kitchen is **0.25–0.32 g salt per 100 g of finished dish**.

| Source | Salt |
|-------:|-----:|
| Dough | 4.0 g |
| Onion layer | 1.0 g |
| Tau kwa, inherent (0.26 g/100 g × 200 g) | 0.5 g |
| **Total** | **5.5 g** |

Estimated finished mass **~1870 g** → **0.29 g salt per 100 g**. Mid-band.

- **The whole umami stack is salt-free: 5 g nutritional yeast (glutamate) + 4 g shiitake powder (guanylate).** The `PANTRY.md` warning against nutritional yeast — that its cheesy note fights aromatic-led bases — does not apply here. This dish is dairy-adjacent by construction and the cheesy note is an asset.
- **No mushroom seasoning.** At 32 g salt per 100 g, 10 g of it would be 3.2 g of salt and would take the whole budget on its own.

## What the test run has to answer

1. **Can a square be picked up by hand after four hours at room temperature?** This is the go/no-go for the school, not the flavour.
2. **Does the custard weep?** If it does, the mushroom pre-drying did not go far enough and phase 1 needs extending.
3. **Does it read as Zwiebelkuchen to Anja**, or just as an onion tart with spices in it? If the answer is the second one, the cumin on top is not doing enough work and the fix is more of it, not more spice in the onions.
4. **Tray yield**, for scaling to however many trays the event needs.
5. **Does the vital wheat gluten earn its place, or does 100% atta hold together on its own?** Answered by pressing the two doughs side by side on the one tray — see the base section. Both halves are white-flour-free either way.

## Chestnuts — considered, not adopted

Raised 2026-11-02. **Two different ingredients, opposite answers.**

**Water chestnuts: no.** Over 70% water, and they release it. A custard-bound tart is the worst possible place for that — it is the same failure the two-phase mushroom prep exists to prevent. Their only property is crunch, which does not survive 40 minutes at 200 °C.

**Sweet chestnuts (Maronen): viable only now that the acid is fixed, and still not adopted.**

| For | Against |
|:----|:--------|
| Strongest German Christmas signal available after the Kümmel swap — a Weihnachtsmarkt item | **Sweet**, and the bake is already carrying 1200 g of sweated onion |
| **Starchy, not watery** — one of the few additions with no moisture penalty | Soft and crumbly, so they compete with 🧊 and 🍄 for the discrete-pieces slot and lose |
| ~2% fat, ~200 kcal/100 g — unlike any other nut, they add substance without richness | Bridge nothing toward the masala side |

**If they go in:** ~150 g cooked, crumbled coarse, **scattered over the onion layer before the custard** — never diced into the Speck mix, which is not the job they can do. ❓ Not in `PANTRY.md`, and vacuum-packed cooked chestnuts are an import item here.

The reason they are now *possible* is the acid fix above: sweet against acid against savoury is a working combination, and it is how chestnut earns its place in Rotkohl mit Maronen. Without acid in the dish it was sweetness piled on sweetness.
