# Peanut–Kecap Asparagus & Sprout Bowl (Gado-Gado-Style)

*A quick solo bowl Holger threw together and rated **very good**: lightly steamed asparagus and bean sprouts tossed in a loose **defatted-peanut-flour + kecap manis** sauce, with **capers** supplying the sharp brine-acid that cuts the sweet-rich peanut-soy base. Essentially a deconstructed **gado-gado** — peanut sauce over veg + sprouts — with capers standing in for the tamarind/lime the cuisine normally uses. High-protein, near-fat-free (defatted flour), bright. Single serving. **Quantities are estimates from an unmeasured cook — fill the Actual column when you remake it.***

## Method

1. **Sauce.** Whisk the 🥜 peanut flour with the 🍯 kecap manis and a splash of warm water (plus a little reserved 🫒 caper brine) into a loose, pourable sauce — it should read sweet-salty-nutty with a briny edge.
2. **Asparagus.** Steam or blanch the 🥬 asparagus 2–3 min, kept crisp (or leave raw if pencil-thin).
3. **Toss** the asparagus, 🌱 sprouts and 🫒 capers through the sauce.
4. **Finish** with a squeeze of 🍋 lime if it needs lifting. **The acid is the point** — without enough sharpness the peanut-kecap base turns cloying.

## Design notes — why it works

- **It's a deconstructed gado-gado.** Peanut sauce over blanched veg *and* bean sprouts is the canonical structure; the sprouts aren't incidental.
- **Peanut + kecap manis = a sweet-salt-umami-fat block that is cloying on its own.** Every authentic version cuts it with a sharp sour note (tamarind in the bumbu, lime over gado-gado). Here the **capers** do that job — brine + acid + a mustardy pungency — a non-Indonesian ingredient filling the cuisine's structural need.
- **Defatted peanut flour keeps it lean** (vs satay's fat), which makes the acid read even sharper and the dish feel light rather than rich.
- **Next-time acids** (all stocked): lime (fresh/bright), tamarind (deeper/fruitier), rice vinegar (cleaner). Each tilts it differently.
