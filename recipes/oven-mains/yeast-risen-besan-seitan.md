# Yeast-Risen Besan Seitan [09-11-26] ✴️

*Seitan built to be **fluffy rather than chewy**, which means almost every instruction is the reverse of the usual one. The 🌾 vital wheat gluten is deliberately diluted with 🫘 besan and 🫓 atta instead of standing alone; the dough is **barely mixed** instead of kneaded hard; the hydration is roughly 1 : 1 against the dry weight; and it is **baked in moist heat, never simmered or pressure-cooked**. 🫧 Yeast does the lifting, and the 🫓 atta is in the formula for its enzyme rather than its flavour.*

> ⚠⚠ **This is the opposite recipe to [`pressure-steamed-seitan-sausage.md`](./pressure-steamed-seitan-sausage.md), on purpose.** That one says *"knead hard, not gently"* and pressure-steams for maximum density. **This one says do not knead, and pressure is the thing that would ruin it.** Read the title before the method — the two are not variations of each other and following the wrong one will produce the wrong food.

> ⚠ **PLANNED, NOT COOKED.** No quantity here has been weighed and the yield is an estimate. The *Actual used* column is empty on purpose — see **Weigh-ins** under Design notes.

## Method

### 1. Besan — the pan, first

1. **Dry-roast the 🫘 besan**, no oil, 4–5 minutes over medium heat, stirring, until it smells nutty and darkens slightly.
   > ⚠ **Light, not dark.** Hard roasting denatures the chickpea protein and costs binding power. That matters little here because the 🌾 gluten is the structure, but there is nothing to gain by overshooting.
   > ⚠ Raw besan tastes bitter and chalky and **must** be cooked out. The 70-minute bake would get there on its own; roasting also removes the beany note and deepens the flavour, so it is worth the five minutes.
2. ⚠⚠ **Let it cool to room temperature before it goes anywhere near the yeast.** Besan comes off the pan well over 100 °C. Tipped straight into the mix it will kill the 🫧 yeast outright, and the loaf will not rise at all. This is the single easiest way to waste the whole bake.

### 2. Dough — barely worked

3. **Combine all the dry**: 🌾 gluten, the cooled 🫘 besan, 🫓 atta, 🥔 potato starch, 🧀 nooch, 🫧 instant yeast, 🍬 sugar and 🧂 salt. Mix them dry and thoroughly, so the yeast is distributed before any water arrives.
4. **Add the wet** — ~350 g warm 💧 water, 🥑 oil, 🦪 oyster sauce and 🍥 dashi powder — and **mix for 30–60 seconds only, to a shaggy mass.**
   > ⚠⚠ **Do not knead.** At 70% protein the gluten hydrates and develops almost instantly; kneading only tightens an already over-strong network, and a tight network resists the gas instead of inflating. This is the exact instruction that is reversed in the sausage recipe.
5. **Rest 20 minutes, covered.** The highest-value step in the method and it costs nothing but clock time: the gluten relaxes and extensibility climbs, which is what lets it expand later.
6. **Three gentle coil folds, ten minutes apart.** Lift the dough from one side, fold it over itself, turn the bowl, repeat. This builds strength without tightening — the same reason bread dough is folded rather than kneaded.

### 3. Proof

7. **Shape loosely into an oiled loaf tin, cover, and leave real headroom** — the tin should be about half full.
8. **Proof 90 minutes.** Singapore ambient is already the right temperature; no warm spot or proving box is needed.
   > ⚠ **Expect about 50%, not double.** A gluten-dominant dough will never rise like bread. If it has visibly domed and feels airy when pressed, it has worked.

### 4. Bake

9. **175 °C, 70 minutes, covered, in a bain-marie** — tin sealed tightly with foil, standing in a larger tray holding ~2 cm of hot water.
   > ⚠⚠ **Not the Instant Pot.** Its Steam program pressurises, and pressure is precisely what crushes the crumb the proof just built. A hob steamer is a fine alternative; **simmering or submerging is not** — water logs the crumb and collapses the gas cells hydraulically.
   > Moist heat also keeps the surface from setting into a crust before the inside has finished expanding. Gluten denatures at roughly 60–70 °C, so the window for any oven spring is short.

### 5. Cool — not optional

10. **Cool completely in the tin, ideally overnight in the fridge.** Hot seitan is gummy and tears when cut; the protein keeps firming as it drops through room temperature. A loaf sliced warm will read as a failure that is actually just impatience.
11. **Slice, then use as any cooked protein** — pan-fried, griddled, or cubed into a curry or a stir-fry.

## Design notes

- **Yeast is the fourth-biggest lever on fluffiness, not the first, and the formula reflects that.** Ranked by effect: (1) dilute the gluten so the network is interrupted rather than continuous, (2) hydrate far higher than a sausage dough, (3) use moist heat and never submerge, (4) leaven, (5) barely work it. The 🫧 yeast earns its place for the fine even crumb and the flavour, but on its own in pure gluten it would do very little.
- **🫓 Atta is in this recipe for α-amylase, not for flavour, and it cannot be left out.** Yeast ferments maltose and simple sugars; it has **no amylase of its own and cannot eat starch.** Vital wheat gluten is made by *washing* dough — the starch and every water-soluble enzyme go down the drain — so the 15 g/100 g of residual carbohydrate on the label is trapped starch with nothing left to convert it. The 🫓 atta brings the enzyme back. **Swapping the atta out for more 🫘 besan would undo the rise**, because besan brings no useful amylase either.
- **🍬 Sugar covers the first half hour.** Amylase takes time to produce anything; 8 g of sugar is food the yeast can use immediately, so fermentation starts at once rather than after a lag. It is 1% of the dry weight and contributes nothing to sweetness.
- **🫘 Besan and 🥔 potato starch tenderise by different mechanisms, which is why both are here.** Besan interrupts the gluten network but then **sets on its own, by protein coagulation** — firm, not soft. Potato starch gelatinises and stays soft and contributes no protein structure at all. Using only one of them gives up half the effect.
- **The gluten is 70% of the dry weight, not 100%.** Bread rises because gluten is only 10–13% of flour, diluted in starch into a thin extensible film. At 100% it is a rubber band that resists gas rather than holding it. 70% is a compromise: still unmistakably seitan, but no longer fighting the leavening.
- **Hydration is roughly 1 : 1 against dry weight**, far wetter than the 300 g-gluten sausage dough. Water is what makes gluten extensible rather than merely strong, and an under-hydrated dough cannot inflate however long it proofs.
- **What this will and will not be.** It takes seitan from *rubbery* to *bread-like and spongy*. It will not be brioche. A 70%-protein dough has a ceiling, and anything markedly lighter than this is diluting the gluten much further and calling the result seitan.
- **Considered and not used:** chemical leavening alongside the yeast (baking powder is not recorded in the kitchen, and a second leavener would confound the first cook's reading of whether the yeast worked); whipped egg white or aquafaba folded in (effective, but it is a different dish and it abandons the question actually being asked); and the washed-flour method, which gives a naturally lighter gluten but takes two days and there is vital wheat gluten in the cupboard.

### Weigh-ins for the first cook

| Measurement | Why |
|:------------|:----|
| **Dough into the tin → loaf out of the oven** | The ~710 g yield is a guess. No sealed-bake loss figure exists anywhere on file, and a covered bain-marie should lose far less than the ~45% of an open sheet-pan bake. |
| **Tin volume, and how full it is at shaping** | "About half full" is the instruction; a number makes it repeatable and makes the rise measurable. |
| **Dough height at shaping → height at the end of the proof** | The only way to know whether the 🫧 yeast actually worked, and the whole point of the design. Two ruler readings. |
| **Does it still read as seitan?** | The go/no-go, and not a number. If 70% gluten has tipped it into savoury bread, the next version moves back up rather than further down. |
| **70% gluten vs 80%** | The obvious follow-up: 290 g gluten against 250 g, everything else held. Tells us which side of the compromise this family wants. |
