# Pressure-Steamed Seitan Sausage

*A homemade seitan "sausage" built to run down the stocked 🌾 BWFO vital wheat gluten (Seitan Flour), a glut of 🧅 onions, and both 🌽 corn flour and 🍚 rice flour. 🧅 Onion is pureed straight into the dough, which is kneaded hard (unlike bread, aggressive gluten development is the goal), rolled into a log, wrapped tightly in foil and **pressure-steamed** — sitting on the trivet above the water, never submerged, so all the seasoning has to be kneaded into the dough itself. Cooled, sliced, dipped in 🍶 tamari and dredged in a 🌽🍚 flour blend, then air-fried hot for a crisp crust over an already fully-cooked interior. Serves 4 as a protein component alongside rice/grain and vegetables.*

## Method

> **Knead hard, not gently.** Unlike bread dough, you *want* aggressive gluten development here — the dough should fight back and tighten under your hands. **Never use immediate/quick release on the Ninja** — this recipe uses delayed release throughout.

1. **Blend the onion.** Blitz the 🧅 onion to a smooth puree — ~1-2 min. The moisture and sweetness go straight into the dough.
2. **Mix and knead hard.** Combine the 🌾 seitan flour, the onion puree, 60 g of the 🍶 tamari, and the ~90 ml 💧 water in a bowl. Bring together into a shaggy mass, then knead hard for 5-8 min until it tightens and resists — that resistance is the gluten network developing, not a sign to stop.
3. **Shape and wrap.** Roll into a tight log (~20 cm). Wrap firmly in foil, twisting both ends hard like a sweet-wrapper — a tight wrap is what holds the cylindrical shape through pressure-cooking.
4. **Pressure-steam.** Add ~500 ml water to the Ninja's base and set the trivet in — the log sits **on the trivet, above the water, not submerged**, so no broth flavour gets through the sealed foil (all the seasoning had to go into the dough at step 2). Seal — **high pressure, 30 min, delayed release** (~10 min).
5. **Cool before slicing.** Leave the log wrapped and resting until cool enough to handle, about 15 min. Slicing it warm tears the crumb; cooled, it cuts clean.
6. **Slice.** Unwrap and cut into rounds, ~1.5 cm thick.
7. **Dip and dredge.** Dip each round in the remaining 20 g 🍶 tamari, then dredge through a mixed 🌽 corn flour + 🍚 rice flour dish — the wet dip both seasons the crust and gives the dry coating something to cling to.
8. **Air-fry to crisp.** Toss with the 🥑 oil (or a light spray), single layer, uncrowded. High heat, ~200-220C, ~8-12 min, flipping once — the interior is already fully cooked, so this stage is pure crisping, not doneness.
9. **Serve** sliced, alongside rice/grain and vegetables — the crisp dredge crust is the point, so serve it straight from the basket rather than holding it.

## Design notes

- **"Pressure-steamed" means on the trivet, not submerged.** The log sits above the water line inside sealed foil, so it cooks in steam heat but no liquid — and no broth flavour — reaches the dough. Every bit of seasoning (the 🧅 onion, 60 g 🍶 tamari) has to be kneaded in before wrapping; this is the opposite of a simmered seitan, which picks up flavour from its cooking broth as it goes.
- **Knead hard — the opposite instinct from bread.** Bread dough is handled gently to avoid toughness; seitan wants the reverse. Aggressive kneading develops the gluten network fast and dense, which is what gives pressure-cooked seitan its characteristic chew.
- **Settings are from memory, not yet re-confirmed.** Holger recalled a previous similar cook as roughly high pressure / 30 min / ~10 min delayed release, "not 100% certain." That's what's written here — flagged for correction on the first actual cook if the log comes out under- or over-set. Two genuine historical seitan products turned up in `examples/` while researching this (`Seitan.foodnoms` — Biona ready-made seitan, 101 kcal/100 g; `Seitan-Fix.foodnoms` — Arche Naturprodukte's pre-seasoned vital-wheat-gluten mix, 367 kcal/100 g) but neither carries method/timing data, and the Arche product is a seasoned mix rather than the plain BWFO gluten flour used here, so they don't resolve the settings question — just confirm the general "vital wheat gluten → pressure-cooked seitan" family is a known technique in this household.
- **Wet-dip-then-dry-dredge is a coating technique, not a flavour delivery mechanism for the interior.** By the time the log is sliced, the inside is fully cooked and fully seasoned (from the dough); the tamari dip + flour dredge only builds the crust that the air fryer then crisps.
- **Runs down four stocked items at once**, per the pantry rundown: 🌾 BWFO Seitan Flour, 🧅 onions, 🌽 corn flour, 🍚 rice flour — no new purchases.
- **Salt is concentrated, not diluted like a soup or sauce.** ~11.2 g across ~655 g of dense protein reads very differently from the same salt spread through a 2+ kg pot — worth a small first slice to taste before committing to the full tamari quantity on a repeat cook.
