# Super-Quick Hasselback Jacket Potatoes

*A pipelined jacket potato: **hasselback-cut raw with a knife**, **microwaved through in pairs** so the dense centre cooks in minutes (the slow part of a 60-90 min oven bake), then **air-fried at 240C** so the fanned slices crisp into golden ribs in one short blast. The two stations are rate-matched — a ~6 min microwave pair hands straight to a ~6 min air-fry batch — so it runs as a tight 1:1 line with neither waiting on the other. Just potato, oil (sprayed into the cuts) and salt — nothing bought, kid-safe, and mild. Toppings at the table so everyone builds their own. Family of five, one ~220 g potato each; ~30 min end to end over paired batches (less if your basket fits more per layer). Calibrated for Holger's kit: a 700 W microwave and the Ninja air fryer at its 240C top.*

## Method

> **Depth-stop for the cut.** Lay each potato between two chopsticks (or wooden-spoon handles) so the knife hits them and can't slice all the way through — the base stays intact and the potato fans instead of falling apart. **Floury baking potatoes** (russet, Maris Piper, King Edward) crisp and fluff best; waxy salad potatoes stay dense and won't fan.

1. **Hasselback cut — raw.** Scrub the 🥔 potatoes (skin on). Sitting each between the chopsticks, slice straight down at ~3-4 mm intervals along its length, the blade stopping on the chopsticks so the bottom stays joined. Cutting **raw** matters — a firm potato slices into clean, defined ribs; a softened one tears. ~3-4 min for all five. Start the air fryer preheating to **240C** now.
2. **Microwave in pairs, full power.** Two ~220 g potatoes at a time on full 700 W, **~5.5-6 min a pair**, until a knife slides into the centre with **no resistance** (do a single potato in ~3 min). Swap their positions once partway — two potatoes shadow each other, so the sides facing out cook faster. Rest 1 min; residual steam finishes the middle. Keep the pairs coming to feed the fryer (step 4). *(Times are for 700 W; scale inversely with your wattage. The knife test is the arbiter.)*
3. **Spray oil and salt into the cuts.** An **oil sprayer/mister is the right tool** — a few passes drive ~3 g 🥑 avocado oil per potato (**15 g total**) straight **down into the fanned cuts**, coating the slice edges with far less oil than brushing. Fan the slices apart as you spray so it reaches between them — that edge contact is what crisps. 🧂 Salt generously (~1 g each).
4. **Air-fry to crisp — 240C, keep the basket fed.** Into the preheated basket, **240C, ~6 min per batch**, single layer and uncrowded (crowding steams instead of crisps). The potatoes are already cooked, so the heat does one job — crisp the fanned edges and brown the surface. **Check at ~4 min**: at 240C the thin edges brown fast, so pull when they're golden, not mahogany (drop to ~190C and add a minute if they threaten to scorch before the faces crisp). Feed the next pair in as it comes off the microwave so the fryer never idles.
5. **Serve.** Straight from the basket, fans up. Put toppings on the table so everyone customises — 🧀 nutritional yeast, 🌿 chives/spring onion, butter, cottage cheese, or warmed beans. Keep sour or smoked toppings (sour cream, smoked paprika) at the table and off Lara's.

## Design notes

- **Microwave cooks, air fryer crisps — that's the whole trick.** A traditional oven jacket spends 60-90 min mostly getting heat into the dense centre. The microwave does that in ~3 min per potato; the air fryer is then left with the one job it's fast at — crisping the outside — which at 240C is one ~6 min blast. Total ~30 min instead of ~75.
- **Rate-matched pipeline — run the microwave flat out.** The stations are balanced by luck of the numbers: a pair of 220 g potatoes microwaves in ~5.5-6 min at full 700 W, and the air fryer crisps a batch in ~6 min at 240C. That's a near-perfect 1:1 line, so you **run the microwave at full power** and just hand each pair to the fryer — no dialling the microwave down. (Dialling down only helps when the fryer is the slower stage with idle microwave capacity to spend on even cooking; here the fryer is quick, so there's no slack — full power it is.) The one rule: microwave **1-2 at a time, never all five** — a microwave's power is fixed, so a full plate splits it and cooks slowly and unevenly.
- **240C because it's already cooked.** With no raw centre to worry about, air-fryer temperature is a pure crisping lever, so it goes to the top of the range — fastest Maillard on the fanned edges in the shortest window. Avocado oil's ~270C smoke point covers 240C with margin (spray, don't pool). Watch the thin slice edges — they brown fast at 240C.
- **Cut raw, not after microwaving.** A raw potato is firm and slices into clean, defined hasselback ribs; a microwaved one is soft and tears. The chopstick depth-stop keeps the base joined. Cutting first also *speeds the microwave* (more surface) and pre-opens the fans so oil gets in.
- **Oil sprayer for the cuts.** A mister drives oil down between the fanned slices and coats every edge with less oil than brushing or drizzling — and edge contact is what crisps. (Holger uses a sprayer as standard.)
- **Floury over waxy.** Baking potatoes (russet / Maris Piper / King Edward) go fluffy inside and crisp at the edges. Waxy salad potatoes stay dense and won't fan or crisp.
- **Fat is 3 g per potato** (15 g across five) — enough to crisp the ribs, in line with Holger's low-fat default. For a solo potato, 3 g total keeps it at his ≤3 g/dish.
- **Pantry run-down + kids.** Base is just oil + salt — nothing bought, nothing fermented, no heat: green for Jannes and Lara. All flavour is at-table and per-person (nutritional yeast is in stock; butter/cottage cheese/beans/chives as available), so acidic or smoked toppings stay off Lara's plate by choice, not by cooking two versions.
