# Super-Quick Hasselback Jacket Potatoes

*A synchronized jacket potato: **hasselback-cut raw with a knife**, **microwaved through in two back-to-back pairs** so the dense centre cooks in minutes (the slow part of a 60-90 min oven bake), then **all four air-fried together in one 240C batch** so they land on the table at the same moment. Both microwave pairs run first — trading a little of the pipelined version's throughput for a genuinely simultaneous finish — then the whole tray hits the preheated fryer at once. Just potato, oil (sprayed into the cuts) and salt — nothing bought, kid-safe, and mild. Toppings (or the **[Creamy Tomato, Coconut & Spinach Sauce](../stovetop-mains/creamy-tomato-coconut-spinach-sauce.md)**) at the table so everyone builds their own. Four potatoes, one ~220 g each; ~23 min end to end. Calibrated for Holger's kit: a 700 W microwave and the Ninja ML750 air fryer at its 240C top.*

## Method

> **Depth-stop for the cut.** Lay each potato between two chopsticks (or wooden-spoon handles) so the knife hits them and can't slice all the way through — the base stays intact and the potato fans instead of falling apart. **Floury baking potatoes** (russet, Maris Piper, King Edward) crisp and fluff best; waxy salad potatoes stay dense and won't fan.

1. **Hasselback cut — raw, all four.** Scrub the 🥔 potatoes (skin on). Sitting each between the chopsticks, slice straight down at ~3-4 mm intervals along its length, the blade stopping on the chopsticks so the bottom stays joined. Cutting **raw** matters — a firm potato slices into clean, defined ribs; a softened one tears. ~3 min for all four.
2. **Microwave pair 1, full power.** Two ~220 g potatoes at a time on full 700 W, **~5.5-6 min**, until a knife slides into the centre with **no resistance** (do a single potato in ~3 min). Swap their positions once partway — two potatoes shadow each other, so the sides facing out cook faster. *(Times are for 700 W; scale inversely with your wattage. The knife test is the arbiter.)*
3. **Spray + salt pair 1, then microwave pair 2.** While pair 2 goes into the microwave for its own ~5.5-6 min, spray ~3 g 🥑 avocado oil per potato down into pair 1's fanned cuts (an **oil sprayer/mister** drives it between the slices far more evenly than brushing), then 🧂 salt generously (~1 g each). Pair 1 rests, seasoned, while pair 2 cooks.
4. **Preheat the air fryer.** Start it heating to **240C** once pair 2 goes into the microwave — a ~6 min preheat lands it hot exactly as pair 2 finishes.
5. **Spray + salt pair 2.** Same as step 3, the moment pair 2 is out of the microwave. All four potatoes are now cut, cooked through, oiled and salted.
6. **Air-fry all four together — one batch, 240C.** Into the preheated basket, single layer, uncrowded. **~7 min**, checking at ~5 min — at 240C the thin fanned edges brown fast, so pull them golden, not mahogany (drop to ~190C and add a minute if they threaten to scorch before the faces crisp). *If four don't sit in one uncrowded layer, split into two batches that start together on separate racks/baskets rather than staggering — crowding steams instead of crisping.*
7. **Serve.** Straight from the basket, fans up, all four together. Put toppings on the table so everyone customises — 🧀 nutritional yeast, 🌿 chives/spring onion, butter, cottage cheese, warmed beans, or a spoonful of the **[Creamy Tomato, Coconut & Spinach Sauce](../stovetop-mains/creamy-tomato-coconut-spinach-sauce.md)**. Keep sour or smoked toppings (sour cream, smoked paprika) at the table and off Lara's.

## Design notes

- **Synchronized batch, not pipelined — the trade-off this version makes.** The original 5-potato design staggered air-fry batches to keep the fryer continuously fed off a 1:1 rate match with the microwave, so potatoes finished a few minutes apart. Here both microwave pairs run **first**, back-to-back, and everything crisps together in **one** air-fry batch — costing a couple of minutes of total throughput (the fryer sits idle during the microwave stage) in exchange for all four potatoes landing on the table at the same moment. Worth it for a sit-down family meal; the old pipelined approach is still better if you're feeding people as they arrive.
- **Unconfirmed: does the basket hold four in one layer?** This is the load-bearing assumption of the synchronized design — if the ML750 basket can't fit four hasselbacked potatoes uncrowded, "all four together" isn't achievable in a single batch and the best fallback is two batches **started together** (a second basket/rack) rather than staggered, or accepting a short stagger. Flagged in the cook log to confirm on the first cook.
- **240C because it's already cooked.** With no raw centre to worry about, air-fryer temperature is a pure crisping lever, so it goes to the top of the range — fastest Maillard on the fanned edges in the shortest window. Avocado oil's ~270C smoke point covers 240C with margin (spray, don't pool). Watch the thin slice edges — they brown fast at 240C.
- **Cut raw, not after microwaving.** A raw potato is firm and slices into clean, defined hasselback ribs; a microwaved one is soft and tears. The chopstick depth-stop keeps the base joined. Cutting first also *speeds the microwave* (more surface) and pre-opens the fans so oil gets in.
- **Oil sprayer for the cuts.** A mister drives oil down between the fanned slices and coats every edge with less oil than brushing or drizzling — and edge contact is what crisps. (Holger uses a sprayer as standard.)
- **Floury over waxy.** Baking potatoes (russet / Maris Piper / King Edward) go fluffy inside and crisp at the edges. Waxy salad potatoes stay dense and won't fan or crisp.
- **Fat is 3 g per potato** (12 g across four) — enough to crisp the ribs, in line with Holger's low-fat default. For a solo potato, 3 g total keeps it at his ≤3 g/dish.
- **Pantry run-down + kids.** Base is just oil + salt — nothing bought, nothing fermented, no heat: green for Jannes and Lara. All flavour is at-table and per-person (nutritional yeast is in stock; butter/cottage cheese/beans/chives as available, plus the new coconut-tomato-spinach sauce), so acidic or smoked toppings stay off Lara's plate by choice, not by cooking two versions.
- **Pairs with the [Creamy Tomato, Coconut & Spinach Sauce](../stovetop-mains/creamy-tomato-coconut-spinach-sauce.md).** That sauce's ~11 min active time comfortably fits inside this recipe's ~23 min window (start it once the potatoes are cut and the first microwave pair is running) — the two-dish meal doesn't cost extra wall-clock time.
