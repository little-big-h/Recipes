# Ras el Hanout Spelt with Air-Fried Butternut & Chickpeas

*A North-African spiced grain main: whole spelt and chickpeas pressure-cooked together in plain water (no tomato — acid stalls beans and toughens grain), folded into a ras-el-hanout-bloomed tomato sauce finished off-heat with white miso, and topped with separately air-fried butternut so the squash keeps its sweet, caramelised edges. Built mild for Jannes; harissa + lemon held **at the table** for the adults and to keep acid off Lara's portion. High-carb, high-protein, very high fibre. Family of five.*

## Method

> **Soak.** Soak the 🌾 spelt and 🫛 chickpeas **together overnight** (8 h+) in plenty of cold water; drain before cooking. (Whole spelt berries assumed — see design notes for pearled spelt.)

1. **Pressure-cook the base.** Drained 🌾 spelt + 🫛 chickpeas + ~1200 ml 💧 water in the Ninja — **no 🥫 passata, no salt yet** (tomato acid stalls the chickpeas and toughens the grain; salt later). **High pressure 20 min, natural release.** Don't drain — keep the starchy liquid.
2. **Air-fry the squash.** Toss 🎃 butternut cubes with ~3 g 🥑 oil + a pinch of salt; **200 °C, ~20 min**, shaking once, until caramelised. Single layer — do it in **two batches** rather than crowding (crowding steams instead of browns). Set aside.
3. **Build the sauce.** In the wok, bloom 🧄 garlic powder + 🌰 cumin + 🍛 ras el hanout in the remaining ~3 g 🥑 oil ~30 sec, then **bhuna the 🍅 tomato paste ~1 min** until it darkens (the umami anchor). Add the 🥫 passata; simmer 5–8 min.
4. **Combine.** Fold the cooked 🌾 spelt + 🫛 chickpeas into the sauce with a ladle or two of their reserved 💧 liquid; simmer 3–5 min to meld, loosening to a moist pilaf (not soupy).
5. **Finish off heat.** Whisk in the 🍶 white miso (never boil). Taste and add salt — the base went in unsalted.
6. **Top & serve.** Pile the air-fried 🎃 butternut over the top so it keeps its roasted edges. 🌿 parsley over. 🌶️ harissa + 🍋 lemon **at the table** for the adults; keep Lara's portion acid-free.

## Design notes

- **Base in plain water, tomato after.** The recurring rule — tomato acid stalls bean softening (it bit the chickpeas, soy beans, and lobia peas) and toughens whole grain. Spelt + chickpeas cook clean in plain water; the passata goes in at step 3.
- **One pot, creamy chickpeas accepted.** Soaked chickpeas want 12–14 min, soaked whole spelt 22 min. Cooked together at **20 min HP + natural release**, the chickpeas come out soft-creamy (reads well for the family — like the pinto in the 8.16 lobia and the family-favourite pinto soup) and the spelt lands tender-chewy on natural-release carryover. If you want the chickpeas firmer, seal the spelt alone for an 8-min head start, then add the chickpeas for the final 12 — but the single-seal simplicity is worth the trade.
- **Squash air-fried separately, folded late.** Air-frying caramelises it; folding it in at the end (not simmering it into the sauce) keeps the sweet roasted edges instead of dissolving them. Same move as the lobia butternut and the tikka-curry cauliflower.
- **Umami anchor = ras el hanout bhuna + white miso.** A tomato base goes flat without one (the Indian-shakshuka / pinto-v1 lesson). Bhuna'd tomato paste + bloomed ras el hanout do it during the cook; white miso (off heat) finishes it — North-African spice in the slot tikka filled for the lobia.
- **Pantry run-down fit.** Ras el hanout is in stock and wants using before the move; this dish is built around it. No new purchases — spelt assumed on hand (it's not on the standing pantry list, so confirm).
- **Lara / Jannes.** Mild in the pot; ras el hanout kept modest (12 g across the whole dish) to keep the warm aromatics low — a soft Lara caution, not a firm flag. Harissa + lemon held at the table, keeping heat off Jannes and acid off Lara.
- **Spelt type.** Timings assume **whole spelt berries**, soaked (Nussinow: soaked spelt 22 min HP; 1 cup ≈ 186 g, Holger's measurement). If it's **pearled** spelt, skip the long soak and cut the pressure time to ~12–15 min, or the grain will go mushy.
