# Three-Mushroom Soup with Kombu Dashi [04-11-26] ✴️

*Three mushrooms doing three different jobs, over a kombu dashi held at 60 °C on the Instant Pot's sous vide program. 🍄 Portobello and 🍄‍🟫 shiitake are oven-roasted and blended into the base for colour and depth; 🦪 king oyster is roasted separately and goes on top at the bowl, because blending the one mushroom that holds its texture wastes it. 🥔 White potato is the only starch — purple sweet potato and squash were considered and left out, both too sweet against mushroom. No chilli. 🥬 Spinach, 🥛 soya milk and 🍋 lemon are per-bowl additions, not pot additions, so a big batch reheats as well on day two as on day one. Family of five, with leftovers.*

> ⚠ **PLANNED, NOT COOKED.** Every quantity below is design, not measurement. The *Actual used* column is deliberately empty and should be filled on the first cook — see **Weigh-ins** under Design notes.

## Method

### 1. Dashi — Instant Pot, unattended

1. 🌊 Kombu and 1500 ml cold 💧 into the inner pot. **Lid on and locked** — the manual says to use the pressure lid for temperature stability, and the program uses no pressure.
2. **Sous Vide → Chicken, 60 minutes.** The program's default is 60 °C, which is the dashi target, so nothing needs setting but the time. Stir at the start and once midway: there is no circulation pump and the element is in the base.
3. **Lift the kombu out** before anything approaches a simmer. Boiling it pulls alginates and bitter compounds.
4. **Frozen 🍄‍🟫 shiitake stems straight in, no thawing**, into water already at 60 °C. Another **30 minutes**.
   > ⚠ **They must go in after the bath is hot, never at the start.** Freezing ruptures the cells but does not kill the enzymes: below 50 °C phosphomonoesterase destroys 5′-GMP, and only at 60–70 °C does ribonuclease make it. This machine preheats slowly, so stems added cold would sit in the destruction band the whole way up.
5. **Strain.** A mesh bag around the stems beforehand saves fishing woody bits out of 1.5 L.

### 2. Mushrooms — oven, two phases

6. **Preheat to 220 °C, convection with fan**, with the trays in. Bare metal, **not baking paper and not a rack** — paper traps a film of liquid underneath and the mushrooms steam; a rack lets the liquor drip away and scorch, and that liquor carries the glutamate and the nucleotides.
7. 🍄 Portobello and 🍄‍🟫 shiitake caps in thick slices, 🦪 king oyster in 1.5–2 cm coins, spread out over **two trays**. Crowding dominates every other variable.
8. **Dry phase, no oil, ~20 min**, until the cut faces go from wet to matte.
9. **Toss with 10 g 🥑 oil, back in ~12 min** until browned. Oil only once the water has gone.

### 3. Aromatics — small wok

10. 🧅 Onion in 10 g 🥑 oil, **browned properly** — unlike a Zwiebelkuchen, colour is wanted here.
11. **Deglaze the wok with a ladle of the strained dashi** and pour it into the Instant Pot. The fond is the reason for browning; leaving it in the wok throws that away.

### 4. Base

12. Into the Instant Pot: the dashi, the deglazed onions, 🥔 potato, and the roasted 🍄 portobello and 🍄‍🟫 shiitake. **Sauté → Custom 1 (95 °C)**, about 20 min, until the potato gives.
    > ⚠ 95 °C is below boiling and is the right setting: the element is a ring in the base and a thick purée catches. Keep it loose, stir, and never try to thicken by reduction in this pot. ⚠ Sauté times out at 1 hour — restart if needed.
13. **Blend smooth.** Add the 🧂 salt here, where you can taste it against the finished base.

### 5. At the bowl, not in the pot

14. 🥬 Spinach wilted through off the heat — blended spinach turns a brown soup khaki. **Keep the stems and cut them small**: puay leng stalks stay crunchy when cut short and added off the heat, which is a second texture for free. ⚠ Eat promptly — a bowl standing ten minutes softens them. See `../../docs/TECHNIQUES.md` → *Puay leng stems*, which also records that puay leng is true spinach despite the "Chinese spinach" label.
15. 🥛 Soya milk **off the heat, never boiled after**.
16. 🍋 Lemon **after** the soya milk, or at the table.
    > ⚠ These two conflict and the order matters: soy protein flocculates near pH 4.5. In a 3 kg pot buffered by potato and mushroom a squeeze will not get near that, but lemon at the table is both safer and fresher.
17. 🦪 King oyster on top.

## Design notes

- **Three mushrooms, three jobs.** 🍄‍🟫 Shiitake is the aroma and the guanylate; 🍄 portobello is the colour and the depth, gills left on because a dark soup is wanted; 🦪 king oyster is the texture and is the only one not blended.
- **Kombu completes the pair.** Shiitake supplies guanylate, kombu supplies glutamate, and the two multiply rather than add. Building the stock from real kombu rather than the dashi powder also keeps the stock salt-free, which is where the salt budget came from.
- **What was left out, and why.** Purple sweet potato and squash are both sweet, and sweetness is what flattens mushroom; the purple would also turn a brown soup grey-violet. A yam was available and would have given a silkier body than potato if it is the Chinese 淮山 — unconfirmed, so it is not in the plan.
- **Lemongrass and makrut lime leaves were also to hand and are deliberately absent.** `../../docs/TECHNIQUES.md` → *keep umami in-cuisine*: the stock here is kombu and shiitake, a Japanese build, and lemongrass is assertive enough to overwrite it. Holding a dashi at 60 °C for an hour to get a clean subtle stock and then putting a citrus-piney aromatic through it wastes the effort. Makrut fills no gap either — the acid is the 🍋 at the table, and makrut gives aroma rather than acidity. ⚠ There is also a lineage argument: galangal-and-lemongrass over a sweet starchy base is already two soups on file (the 30-10 purple sweet potato soup and `corn-galangal-bamboo-soup.md`), and this one was designed to be a different thing. **Where they would earn their place is lemongrass *instead of* the kombu, making a Thai mushroom broth — a replacement for this design, not an addition to it.**
- **Scaling is limited by the mushrooms, not the pot.** For this to read as mushroom soup rather than potato soup, roasted mushroom wants to be 12–15% of the finished mass, and roasting costs about 35%. **Maximum soup ≈ raw mushroom into the base × 4.** Here that is 600 g × 4 ≈ 2.4 kg of base; the rest of the 3.3 kg is the per-bowl additions. ⚠ The Instant Pot's `PC MAX — 2/3` line caps *pressure* cooking at 3.8 L, but this recipe never pressure-cooks, so the real ceiling is about 4.5 L for stirring and blending room.
- **Why spinach and soya milk are per-bowl.** A batch this size gets reheated two or three times. Soya milk curdles on repeated heating, spinach goes khaki and slimy, and a concentrated base takes less fridge space. Day two is then as good as day one.
