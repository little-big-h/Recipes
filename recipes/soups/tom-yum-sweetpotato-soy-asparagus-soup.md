# Tom Yum Sweet Potato & Soy Soup with Asparagus

*A creamy soup with **no stock at all** — the liquid is a full litre of 🥛 unsweetened soy milk, thickened with blended 🍮 silken tofu and roasted 🍠 sweet potato, sharpened by 🍲 Mae Ploy Vegan Tom Yum Paste. 🌿 Steamed asparagus and 🍅 roasted cherry tomatoes go in whole at the end for texture and colour. **Everything is cooked out of the pot** — the sweet potato and tomatoes roast, the asparagus steams — which concentrates their flavour instead of leaching it into water, and keeps two acidic ingredients away from the soy until the very end. ⚠ The dish's central hazard is that **soy protein curdles in acid**, and the paste is 5.3% citric acid; the whole method is built around that. Serves 5 with leftovers.*

## Method

> ⚠ **The one rule that governs this recipe: soy curdles in acid.** 🍲 Tom yum paste is 5.3% citric acid and 6.4% tamarind, and 🍅 tomatoes are acidic too. Soy protein flocculates around pH 4.5 and is far more acid-sensitive than dairy. So **both the 🥛 soy milk and the 🍮 tofu go in at the blender, off the heat, and nothing boils afterwards** — and the 🍅 tomatoes never share a pan with the soy until the last minute. Heat *and* acid together do the damage; either alone is largely survivable.

1. **Roast the 🍠 sweet potato.** Peel, cube to ~3 cm, toss with about half the 🥑 oil, spread in a single layer and roast at 200 C for ~35 minutes until the edges caramelise and a cube collapses under a spoon. **Two trays rather than one crowded one** — crowded sweet potato steams, and the roast is the entire reason there is no stock in this recipe.
2. **Roast the 🍅 cherry tomatoes** on a separate tray for the last ~15 minutes, halved, cut side up, until blistered and slightly collapsed. Set aside whole — they are not going in the blender.
3. **Steam the 🌿 asparagus** 3–5 minutes depending on thickness, until just yielding and still bright. **Do not overshoot** — it is the only texture and the only green in the bowl. If it will wait more than a few minutes, refresh it under cold water to hold the colour.
4. **Fry the 🧅 onion** in the remaining 🥑 oil, ~6 minutes, until soft and just colouring.
5. **Fry the 🍲 paste** into it, 3–4 minutes, until it darkens and smells cooked rather than raw. Add the 🍃 makrut lime leaves and a splash of water to deglaze. **This is the only seasoning step in the dish** — there is no stock and no salt behind it.
6. **Blend.** Fish out the 🍃 lime leaves and discard. **Take the pan off the heat.** Combine the roasted 🍠 sweet potato, the 🧅 onion-and-paste mixture, the 🍮 silken tofu and the 🥛 soy milk in the blender and blend until completely smooth. Blending is what protects the soy: it disperses the acid instantly and evenly instead of letting it hit the milk in a hot, concentrated pocket.
7. **Warm gently.** Return to the pan over low heat, just to serving temperature. **Never let it simmer**, let alone boil.
8. **Fold in** the steamed 🌿 asparagus off the heat, along with the 🍄 mushroom powder. **Taste before adding any salt at all.**
9. **Serve** with the roasted 🍅 tomatoes **in a separate bowl at the table**, to be spooned over, and 🍋 lime wedges alongside.

## Design notes

- **⚠ The soy-curdling problem is the whole reason this method looks the way it does.** Soy protein flocculates near pH 4.5 and is markedly less acid-tolerant than dairy; the 🍲 paste is **5.3% citric acid and 6.4% tamarind**, and the 🍅 tomatoes bring more. Three defences, in order of importance: (1) **the soy goes in at the blender**, so the acid is dispersed instantly and evenly across the whole volume rather than hitting the milk in a hot concentrated pocket; (2) **nothing boils afterwards** — heat and acid together are what break it, either alone is largely survivable; (3) **the 🍅 tomatoes never share a pan with the soy** until the final off-heat fold. A **barista** soy milk is the belt-and-braces option — those carry stabilisers for exactly this — but the M&S Plant Kitchen carton in use here already contains **gellan gum (E418)**, a stabiliser that does the same job, so it is better protected against splitting than a plain unstabilised soya drink would be. And blending is itself insurance: any micro-curdling disappears into a purée, which would not be true of a brothy version.
- **No stock, and that is an upgrade rather than a shortcut.** Simmering sweet potato in water leaches flavour into liquid you then have to season back up. Roasting concentrates it and adds caramelisation the pot can't. Removing the stock also removes the last thing competing with the soy milk for volume, so the dish reads creamy rather than watery. The cost is that **the 🍲 paste is now the only savoury element behind a litre of bland liquid** — which is why the 🍄 mushroom powder goes up to 8 g.
- **🍲 Paste at 50 g — Holger's call, taken against my recommendation of 40 g, and worth recording with the trade-off intact.** The case for 50 g is straightforward: ~1.3 kg of soy milk and tofu is a lot of bland creamy mass, the paste is the *only* savoury element in a stockless pot, and under-seasoning a soup this large is harder to fix at the table than over-seasoning is to live with. The case against is purely sodium: at 13.9% salt the jar buys seasoning expensively, and 50 g takes the dish to **9.8 g total, 0.30 g per 100 g** — above the 0.26 that the Tom Yum Butternut Curry ran at when it averaged 8.60, and the highest in the soup line. **Both levers stay documented**: 40 g returns it to 0.26, and the salt-free way to add savour is more **guanylate** — the 8 g of dried-mushroom powder here already does that, multiplying against the paste's fermented-soy glutamate at no sodium cost. If a cook reads flat rather than salty, reach for lime and mushroom before the jar.
- **Beaniness is the real flavour risk.** A litre of soy milk plus 300 g of tofu is a lot of soy against lemongrass, and plain soy has an edge that sweet potato will not mask — it will amplify it, since both are mild and sweet. **Unsweetened is mandatory**; a sweetened carton against a 103 g-sugar soup would tip it dessert-ward. Taste before the asparagus goes in.
- **🌿 Asparagus at 600 g is a component, not a garnish.** It is the only texture, the only green and the only bitter note in a soup that is otherwise smooth, sweet and creamy — the same structural job the crisp cauliflower did in the Tom Yum Butternut Curry, which is the build Anja scored 8.9. Steam rather than boil, and stop early: overcooked asparagus goes drab and stringy and there is nothing else to carry the bowl.
- **🍅 Cherry tomatoes are in-style, not an addition — and serving them separately is the better call.** Tomatoes are standard in tom yum. Roasting rather than simmering blisters the skins for sweetness and keeps them whole; **serving them in a bowl at the table rather than folding them through** (as cooked, 2026-08-05) does three further things: they stay hot and intact instead of disappearing into a smooth orange soup, they keep a visual and textural identity the blended base cannot give them, and — the technical one — **their acid never meets the soy at all**, which closes the last curdling risk in the recipe rather than deferring it to the final fold. It also lets anyone skip them.
- **Family handling.** Chilli and lime stay off the pot: the jar's own chilli is mild at 40 g across 3.25 kg, and 🍋 lime goes on the table for Lara's no-in-pot-acid rule. Note that rule is only partly satisfied — the paste carries citric acid regardless (see `docs/RATINGS.md` observation 54, where Lara scored par on a dish with ~2.1 g of built-in citric acid). Everything is blended perfectly smooth, so neither the 🍮 tofu nor any stray curd is visible, which is her other standing constraint.
- **Run-down status:** tom yum paste, makrut lime leaves, seven-mushroom blend and a share of the onion glut. Nothing here requires a purchase.
