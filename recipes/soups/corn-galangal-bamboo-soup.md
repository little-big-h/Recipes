# Corn, Galangal & Bamboo Soup

*A deliberately mild corn soup with all its sharpness assembled in the bowl. It exists because the previous yellow-curry corn soup was **too hot for the children to eat and was never rated** — 180 g of paste in a 4.4 kg pot, dosed high because the jar's sodium was low. This one runs **45 g of combined paste**, below the concentration of the best-rated tom yum in the project, and moves every acid to the table. 🫙 Tamarind, 🥒 German pickled cucumbers and 🥬 kimchi sit alongside so each person builds their own contrast, which also means the pot itself is acid-free — so the 🥥 coconut cannot split and the 🥦 broccoli can cook in the soup and stay green. 🫛 Black-eyed peas are cooked on the stove rather than under pressure, because bite is the point and a pressure cooker makes you commit blind. Family of five.*

## Method

> **Prep:** Dice the 🧅 onion, mince the 🧄 garlic. Break the 🥦 broccoli into florets, then **peel the stems and slice them 3–4 mm** — the stem carries more of the bitterness this soup wants than the florets do. Keep the two piles separate.
>
> ⚠ **None of the 🥦 broccoli is blended.** It goes in whole and late, after the base has come back from the blender.

1. **Cook the 🫛 black-eyed peas, in their own pan, on the stove.** 150 g dry, unsoaked, rinsed, into **750 ml water with about a teaspoon of 🧂 salt**. ⚠ **No acid** — acid stops legume pectin solubilising and leaves the skins chalky rather than firm. Bring to the boil, then **drop to a bare simmer**; a rolling boil tears the skins off and is the main cause of a blown-out pot. **Start tasting at 18 minutes.** They usually want 20–25. Pull them when the skin gives but the centre still resists slightly — that window is two or three minutes wide. **Drain immediately and spread them out**; sitting in hot starchy water will take them past it.
2. **Fry the 🧅 onion and 🧄 garlic** in the 🥑 oil until soft and translucent, 5–6 minutes.
3. **Bloom the pastes — gently, and stop early.** 🍲 HollyFarms, 🍛 SOS yellow and 🫚 galangal paste together, **60–90 seconds on medium**, until it smells cooked and the oil takes colour — **not** until it darkens. **Wet it immediately** with a ladle of the corn liquid. ⚠ The HollyFarms jar is 20 g sugars per 100 g and scorches before the aromatics have cooked out; that rule is the safe one for both pastes, so treat the pair as a single gentle bloom.
4. **Add the cooked 🌽 corn and its liquid** and bring it together, 3–4 minutes. ⚠ Judge the 💧 water here rather than measuring it — the corn arrives with liquid already.
5. **Blend the base.** 🌽 corn and aromatics only. Nothing else has gone in yet, which is the point.
6. **Back in the pot: 🎍 bamboo shoots**, then the 🥦 broccoli — stems first, florets a minute behind, **2–3 minutes total**. They should come out coated and still bright.
7. **Off the heat:** whisk in the 🥥 coconut milk, then the 🌰 shiitake powder and 🍜 dashi powder, then the 🧂 salt. ⚠ **Nothing boils after the coconut goes in.** The Ayam carton has no gums and splits readily under hard heat.
8. **Set the table with the acid.** 🫙 Tamarind diluted in hot water, 🍋 lime wedges, 🥒 pickled cucumbers, 🥬 kimchi, 🌶️ chilli. The 🫛 peas go out in their own bowl.

## Design notes

**The heat dose is the whole point of this recipe, and it is evidence-led.**

| Cook | Paste | Concentration | Outcome |
|:--|:--|--:|:--|
| Tom Yum Broccoli, Carrot & Chickpea, 2026-08-10 | 50 g HollyFarms in 3664 g | **1.37 g/100 g** | **8.45 average.** Jannes 8.0 — his all-time ceiling. No heat complaint |
| Yellow Curry Corn, 2026-09-24 | 180 g SOS in 4400 g | 4.09 g/100 g | **Disqualifying.** Not rated |
| **This recipe** | 15 g HollyFarms + 30 g SOS in 4300 g | **1.05 g/100 g** | — |

It sits *below* the proven concentration rather than at it, because only 15 g of the paste is the calibrated jar. Matching 1.37 by topping up with more SOS would have been arithmetic dressed as evidence. ⚠ **The failure modes are asymmetric**: too hot means the children do not eat, which has already cost one dish; too mild is fixed at the table in thirty seconds.

**Why the acid is all at the table.** Three consequences fall out of it at once, and none of them is about taste:

- 🥦 **The broccoli can go in the pot and stay green.** Acid converts chlorophyll to pheophytin; an acid-free pot is why this soup does not have to steam its broccoli separately the way the tom yum ones do.
- 🥥 **The coconut cannot split.** The Ayam carton has no emulsifier, and acid plus heat is what breaks it.
- 🫛 **The peas stay an option.** Acid blocks legume pectin from solubilising.

**The citrus gap is real and unresolved.** 🌿 Lemongrass paste has run out and FairPrice had neither fresh lemongrass nor makrut lime leaves. 15 g of HollyFarms is 0.35 g/100 g — enough to notice, nowhere near enough to carry a top note across 4.3 kg of sweet corn. 🥒 The pickled cucumbers are doing more of that job than they look like they are, because **dill is a volatile herbal lift** rather than just another sharp edge, and they are proven here — the soybean dinner of 2026-09-30 worked specifically on the mash-plus-pickle combination. ⚠ If only a lime is available, **zest it rather than squeezing it**: the aroma is in the peel oil, and the tamarind already has the acid covered.

**Two moderators of heat, not one.** The previous cook ran 150 g of coconut against a planned 417, and fat is what carries capsaicin off the palate — a lean pot tastes hotter at the same paste dose. This one restores it to 250 g, so the dose and the fat work in the same direction instead of against each other.

**🥬 Kimchi is a table item, not a design element.** `CONTEXT.md` has Jannes as heat-sensitive and preferring less-fermented profiles, and kimchi hits both. Nothing in the pot is flagged for him.

⚠ **Pressure-cooking the corn ahead has a cost.** It drives sweetness out harder than a simmer does — the thermal-degradation case in `TECHNIQUES.md`. If the base reads flat, that is the cause, and the fix is a couple of grams of sugar rather than more salt or more paste.
