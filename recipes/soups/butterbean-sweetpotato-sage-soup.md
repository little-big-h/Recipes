# Butterbean, Sweet Potato & Sage Soup

*A warm, autumnal soup: 🫘 butter beans and 🍠 sweet potato blended into a smooth, naturally creamy gold base — body from the beans' break-down, not added cream or coconut — carried by a sage-led herb note (🌿 dried sage, with a little 🌱 oregano) bloomed into sautéed 🧅 onion and 🧄 garlic. 🎋 Asparagus is kept in ~2–3 cm chunks, seared separately and folded in at the very end so it holds bite and colour against the purée. A finishing whisk of 🍶 white miso for savoury depth (no stock cube). No heat, no smoke, no in-pot acid — clears every family flag; 🍋 lemon and 🌶️ chilli at the table for the adults. Family of five, scaled for leftovers. **A pantry run-down cook — uses up dried sage and oregano; no shopping.**

## Method

> **Note:** No soak needed for the 🫘 butter beans — straight into the pot. Peel and dice 🍠 sweet potato ~3 cm. Halve and slice 🧅 onion, peel 🧄 garlic. Cut 🎋 asparagus into ~2–3 cm chunks, keeping the tips separate (they cook faster). Measure the 🌿 sage and 🌱 oregano out ready to bloom.

1. **Pressure-cook the beans.** Put the 🫘 butter beans in the Ninja with ~1600 ml 💧 water (plain — no salt, no acid: both stall bean softening). Seal — **high pressure, 22 min, natural release.** Don't fill past the max line. Reserve the cooking liquor when done — it's the body of the soup. *(Butter beans unsoaked: 20–25 min HP; 22 min lands them fully soft for a smooth purée.)*

2. **Roast the sweet potato.** While the beans cook, air-fry the 🍠 sweet potato at **200 °C, ~25 min in batches** until soft and caramelising at the edges — 1420 g is more than one basket, so do two loads. Roasting (vs simmering it in) concentrates the sweetness and gives the base a deeper, rounder flavour.

3. **Sauté the aromatics and bloom the herbs.** Heat the 🥑 avocado oil in a pan. Soften the 🧅 onion and 🧄 garlic gently until sweet and translucent (~8–10 min, no colour rush). Turn the heat down, add the 🌿 dried sage and 🌱 dried oregano and stir ~1–2 min in the oil to bloom — dried herbs release far more into warm fat than into water, and sage in particular needs the oil to open up. Take off the heat before the garlic browns.

4. **Sear the asparagus.** In a second hot pan (a little of the reserved bean liquor or a dry pan), sear the 🎋 asparagus chunks ~5–6 min until bright green and just tender with some colour — add the tips for the last ~2 min. Set aside; these go in whole, not blended.

5. **Blend the base.** Combine the cooked 🫘 beans (with their liquor), the roasted 🍠 sweet potato and the bloomed 🧅 onion/🧄 garlic/herb mix in the blender and blend until completely smooth. Loosen with reserved bean liquor or 💧 water to the consistency you want — it should pour, not stand.

6. **Season off the heat.** Return the purée to low heat to warm through. **Off the heat, whisk in the 🍶 white miso** until smooth — never boil miso, it dulls it. Taste: the base is naturally sweet, so it wants a firm savoury hand — add more miso or a little salt if it's flat (see notes; the salt here is deliberately modest).

7. **Fold in the asparagus.** Stir the seared 🎋 asparagus chunks through the finished soup just to warm — keep them intact for bite and colour.

8. Ladle out. 🍋 Lemon wedges and 🌶️ chilli flakes at the table for the adults — brightness and heat kept off the pot for Lara and Jannes.

## Design notes

- **Sage-led, oregano backing — a run-down cook.** Sage + butter beans + sweet potato is a classic autumnal trio: sage's warm, earthy-savoury note is the natural counterweight to the sweet-potato sweetness. Oregano is the punchier, more Mediterranean herb; kept to a supporting 3 g so it deepens without taking over, and to nick down the jar. Both are dried and **bloomed in the oil, not the water** — dried herbs give far more to warm fat, and sage especially needs it to open. (Considered and rejected tonight: a lime-leaf/lemongrass/coconut Thai lane — lovely, but it fights sage and oregano, so it's earmarked for its own cook.)
- **Body from beans, not fat.** 550 g of dried butter beans blend to a smooth, genuinely creamy base — no cream or coconut milk needed. Total fat is just 16 g (mostly the 5 g cooking oil). Same lever as the pinto–butternut soup: legume starch does the work.
- **Roast the sweet potato, don't just boil it in.** Air-frying at 200 °C concentrates the sugars and adds a caramelised depth the simmer-in version misses — worth the extra step and the batching for a soup this size. Simmering it in with the beans is the faster fallback if you're pressed.
- **Asparagus stays whole and goes in last.** Seared separately and folded through at the end so it keeps bite and bright colour against the smooth gold purée — it's the textural and visual contrast. Blending it in would waste it. Tips added late so they don't overcook.
- **Beans cooked in plain water.** No salt, no acid in the pressure pot — both slow bean softening (documented in `../../docs/TECHNIQUES.md`). All seasoning goes in after, off the heat.
- **Miso off the heat.** White miso whisked in after the pan comes off — boiling dulls it. White (not red) is the family default: milder and less salty, and it keeps Jannes happy (he's red-averse). Salt is deliberately modest at ~6.5 g across ten bowls; the sweet base can take more, so taste and lift it with extra miso or a pinch of salt at the pot.
- **Clears every family flag.** No chilli or heat in the pot (Jannes, Lara), no smoke, no cooked peppers, no in-pot acid (Lara) — lemon and chilli are at-table for the adults only. Sweet potato blended smooth also sidesteps Jannes's cool-ness on potatoes — it reads as a creamy soup, not a bowl of potato.
- **Nutrition shape.** High protein (~168 g) and very high fibre (~174 g) for the pot — ~17 g each per bowl. Generous but on the heavy side of fibre; if that's a concern for the kids, a smaller bowl or a splash more water per serving eases it. Potassium and folate are both very high (beans + sweet potato + herbs).
