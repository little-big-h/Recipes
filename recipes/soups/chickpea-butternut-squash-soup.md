# Chickpea & Butternut Squash Soup

*A smooth, golden Indian-style soup: 🫘 dried chickpeas pressure-cooked in the Ninja with 🎃 whole skin-on butternut squash steamed in a second layer above them, then the two blended into a naturally creamy base — body from chickpea and squash, not cream. The flavour comes from a 🥥 coconut-cream bhuna: the solid cap skimmed off a can of light coconut milk is used as the frying fat to bloom 🧅 onion, 🧄 garlic and 🫚 ginger, then 🍅 tomato purée, 🍛 mild curry powder and 🌼 turmeric are fried into it until brick-red, and the whole thing blends into the soup. No chilli, no heat in the pot — kid-safe; 🍋 lemon and 🌶️ chilli at the table for the adults. Steamed 🥦 cauliflower served alongside. Family of five, big pot with leftovers. **A pantry run-down cook — mild curry powder + turmeric, no shopping.**

## Method

> **Note:** No soak for the 🫘 chickpeas. Halve the 🎃 squash, scoop the seeds and stringy cavity, cut into chunks but **leave the skin on** (it purées away and saves peeling) — scrub it first. Slice 🧅 onion, mince 🧄 garlic. Chill the 🥥 coconut milk can if you can, so the cream cap sets firm for skimming.

1. **Pressure-cook the chickpeas + squash together.** Put the 🫘 chickpeas in the Ninja with ~1.2 L 💧 water (plain — **no salt, no acid**; both stall bean softening). Set the rack/basket above the water line and pile the 🎃 squash chunks in it — the second layer steams while the chickpeas boil. Seal — **high pressure, 38 min, natural release.** Reserve the chickpea liquor.

2. **Build the coconut bhuna.** Skim the solid 🥥 cream cap off the can and melt it in a pan over **medium** (coconut fat has a lower smoke point than oil — don't rush it). Soften the 🧅 onion, 🧄 garlic and 🫚 ground ginger in it until sweet. Add the 🍅 tomato purée, 🍛 mild curry powder and 🌼 turmeric and **fry until the paste darkens from bright to brick-red** and smells deep-cooked — that bhuna is the umami anchor (the fat-soluble spice and curcumin bloom into the coconut fat). Splash in a little cooking liquor if it catches.

3. **Steam the cauliflower separately.** 🥦 Cauliflower to crisp-tender in a stovetop steamer (or air-fry for a roasted edge). Hold — it's served **alongside**, not in the soup.

4. **Blend.** Combine the cooked 🫘 chickpeas, the 🎃 squash, the 🥥 bhuna and the thin coconut liquid left in the can in the blender with enough reserved liquor / 💧 water to blend completely smooth. It should pour, not stand.

5. **Season off the heat.** Warm through, then **🧂 salt to taste** — the sweet, starchy base needs a firm hand (landed at ~9 g added; ~10 g total with what's intrinsic). Taste and push it up if it reads flat.

6. Ladle out, with the steamed 🥦 cauliflower on the side. 🍋 Lemon wedges and 🌶️ chilli flakes at the table for the adults — brightness and heat kept off the pot for the kids.

## Design notes

- **One pot, two layers, one purée — the cook-time mismatch is a feature.** Dried chickpeas need ~38 min at pressure; butternut wants 0–3 min. Steaming the squash above the chickpeas for the *full* 38 min would ruin it for a textured dish — but since everything's blended, "collapsed to mush" is exactly the goal. The two wildly different cook times stop mattering the moment you commit to a purée, and you get both done in one energy cycle.
- **Skin-on squash.** Butternut skin is thin and edible; after a 38-min steam it blends smooth, so peeling is pointless here. Keeps the carotene-rich under-skin layer and a little more fibre; the tan skin barely mutes the gold. Just scrub it and still scoop the seeds.
- **Coconut-cream-as-frying-fat (the course trick).** Skim the solid cream cap off the can and use it as the bhuna fat — fat-soluble curry compounds and curcumin bloom far better into fat than water, and frying the tomato purée in it to brick-red builds the umami anchor. The thin coconut liquid left in the can goes into the soup for body. Whole can used, nothing wasted. Works with a can that separates a firm cap (chill it first); the stocked Biona Light does cap despite being 9 % fat.
- **In-cuisine umami — no miso.** This is an Indian pot, so the savoury depth comes from the tomato-purée bhuna, not soy/miso/aminos (which would muddy it — the documented Indian-profile rule). Salt is the seasoning lever, added off the heat.
- **Turmeric for the kids.** Colour and earthy warmth, zero heat — blooms in the coconut fat alongside the curry powder. Held to 7 g (~2 tsp); more turns bitter and stains everything.
- **Chickpeas cooked in plain water.** No salt, no acid in the pressure pot — both slow softening. All seasoning after.
- **Cauliflower split to a side.** Originally planned folded in for texture, but a smooth Indian soup + a separate steamed-cauliflower side reads cleaner, and cauliflower can't ride the 38-min chickpea cook without collapsing anyway. It gets its own `.foodnoms`.
- **Kid-safe by construction.** No chilli or heat in the pot, no smoke, no in-pot acid; the smooth sweet base and mild coconut-curry profile are exactly what landed Jannes at the top of his range (see cook log).
