# Cheesy Roasted Cauliflower Soup [11-11-26] ✴️

*A vegan cream soup that reads genuinely cheesy, and does it with **30 g of nutritional yeast rather than 60** — because the 🍥 shiitake dashi beside it multiplies the nooch instead of competing with it. 🫘 Silken tofu does the body, 🥣 brown miso the fermented depth, and the 🥦 cauliflower is **oven-roasted, not boiled**, which is where the nutty sweetness comes from. **3 g of oil in a 3.7 kg pot.***

> ✅ **COOKED AND RATED 11-11-2026 — average 8.10 across five**: **Jannes 8.5**, Holger 8.3, Anja 8.0, Julina 8.0, Lara 7.7. ⚠⚠ **Jannes's 8.5 is the first score above 8.0 he has given in 21 ratings** — on cauliflower, which he is flagged against, in a soup he cooked himself. See remark 81 and observations 57 and 62 in [`../../docs/RATINGS.md`](../../docs/RATINGS.md).

> **This dish was asked for as a creamy, cheesy, vegan *broccoli* soup and is none of those nouns.** There was no broccoli in the house, so cauliflower — which voided every argument about protecting the green and made oven-roasting the obvious choice over microwave-steaming. Quantities below are **as cooked**.

## Method

### 1. Cauliflower — the oven, and this is the flavour

1. **Break into florets, peel and dice the stalk.** ~1 g 🥑 oil per 100 g. **220 °C, convection with fan, ~35 min**, until deep golden at the edges.
   > ⚠⚠ **Roasting is the entire reason this tastes of anything.** Steamed cauliflower is bland and faintly sulfurous; roasted is nutty and sweet, and those Maillard notes are what the "cheesy" reading is built on top of. Microwaving would have been right for broccoli, for colour reasons that do not exist here.
   > ⚠⚠ **Convection with fan, not a roast mode.** Moving air strips the humid boundary layer off a vegetable that is 92% water and has to shed a third of its weight. A top-element roast browns the upper surface while the florets beneath sit in their own steam.
   > ⚠⚠ **Do not crowd the tray.** On 2026-11-10 a spread tray of roots lost 22% and a crowded one 16% — **and the crowded version comes out pale and steamed.** As cooked, 1010 g went on a single ceramic tray (tare 655 g), which was over a single layer; **split it across two.** Browning is the whole point and a heaped tray gives the weight loss without it.
   > ⚠ **Deep golden, not black.** Burnt brassica goes acrid and there is no sauce here to hide it.

### 2. Onions — small wok, pale

2. **🧅 Onion into the small wok and sweat soft with no colour**, ~25 minutes.
   > ⚠ **Pale, not caramelised.** At 875 g the onion is the largest thing in the pot, and caramelised onion is markedly sweet — which would fight the savoury direction the 🧀 nooch and 🥣 miso are there to build. ✅ **As cooked it needed no oil at all**: 875 g of onion releases enough water to sweat on its own.
3. **🧄 Garlic in for the last 2–3 minutes only.**
   > ⚠ 60 g of garlic will scorch bitter over a 25-minute pale sweat, and bitterness cannot be pulled back out of a blended soup.

### 3. Assemble and blend — off the heat

4. **Dissolve the 🍥 dashi powder in a little hot 💧 water.**
5. **Everything into the pot off the heat**: roasted 🥦 cauliflower, 🧅 onions and 🧄 garlic, 🫘 silken tofu, the 🍥 dashi, 🥣 miso, 🧀 nooch, 🧂 salt, and the 🥛 soya milk **cold**.
   > ⚠⚠ **The 🥛 soya milk is never heated.** Soy milk scorches on a direct base — on file from the Tom Yum Broccoli design. Adding it cold at this point also drops the pot's temperature, which is what lets the next step happen safely.
   > ⚠ **🥣 Miso goes in off the heat.** Boiling it strips the aroma that is the reason to use it.
6. **Blend smooth. Do not reboil.**
   > ⚠⚠ The Fortune silken is GDL-set with tapioca starch; **reheating it after blending is what turns it grainy.** Serve from here.
   > ✅ Blending smooth also satisfies the standing rule for Lara — silken tofu is fine blended and a problem in recognisable pieces.

⚠⚠ **This is not a garnish list, it is the fix for the one weakness the ratings exposed.** The soup is a smooth purée with no texture at all, and that is exactly the profile **Anja marks down** — she gave it **8.0, her lowest of the week**, a day after 9.8 on the most texturally varied dish of the week (observations 61 and 66). **The toppings add back the texture variety she rewards without touching the soup itself**, which is the version **Jannes scored 8.5** on — the first time he has ever gone above 8.0.

**So one pot serves both ends of the spread.** Jannes takes it plain; the adults build it. That is observation 10's build-your-own pattern doing the same job it does at the hotpot, and here it costs nothing but a few bowls of toppings.

✅ **It also answers the open question this recipe was left with.** The design note below records that the lime was specced for the table and never came out — the first dish of that week where table-side acid was not reached for. **It has now been reached for, as lemon.** The soup does want acid; it just wanted it at the bowl rather than in the pot, which is where observation 60 says it belongs.

🌶️ **Sichuan pepper is numbing rather than hot**, so it is not a chilli-heat problem — but it is a strong sensation and it belongs on the adult side of the table. Keeping it there is what lets Jannes's bowl stay the one he scored.

🍅 **The cherry tomatoes are doing more than colour**: tomato glutamate stacks onto the dashi and miso already in the pot, and glutamate and guanylate multiply. That is the same mechanism that made 30 g of nooch taste like twice as much (observation 62).

## Design notes

- ⚠⚠ **30 g of nooch, not 60, and that is the finding.** The design specced 60 g against 12 g of dashi. On the day it went in at **30 g against 32 g of dashi and 30 g of miso**, and the prediction made at that moment was that the soup would read *"savoury-fermented and mushroomy rather than cheesy"*. **It read cheesy — "impressed how cheesy it was. The dashi powder is amazing."** The mechanism was under-weighted rather than unknown: **glutamate and guanylate multiply**, so the shiitake dashi was amplifying the nooch rather than crowding it out. **Rule, now on file as observation 62: with a real dose of guanylate present, halve the nooch rather than raising it.**
- **The cheese role is split three ways, as it was in the squash quiche.** 🥣 Miso for fermented depth, 🧀 nooch for the cheesy note, 🫘 silken tofu for body. No single ingredient does "cheese"; the impression is the three together plus the roasting.
- **Every colour argument from the broccoli version is void here.** Cauliflower is white, so there is no chlorophyll to protect: no uncovered-pot rule, no race off the heat, no fast cool-down, and no penalty for using beige soya milk as the bulk liquid. Switching vegetable simplified the dish rather than complicating it.
- ⚠ **Jannes cooked it, and it broke his ceiling.** Three candidate causes and one meal cannot separate them: the mild aromatic profile, the smooth creamy register that remark 66 already had as his best, and cooking it himself. **The aromatic one was predicted two days in advance** (observation 61, from the Tuesday bowls) and so carries the most weight — but the ownership effect is untested and worth watching.
- **Considered and rejected on the day:** 🥬 **daikon** (95% water, will not brown, a second brassica adding sulfur rather than balance, and nothing about it points at cheesy — better in a dashi broth or hotpot); 🫚 **galangal paste** (pine and camphor against a lactic-umami target, and squarely the aromatic register observation 61 says costs Jannes — it went to Thursday's yellow curry instead).
- ✅ **The acid question is answered, two days late.** Lime was specced for the table and was not used on the night, which made this the only dish that week where table-side acid was not reached for — and the note here said it was worth trying once. **It was, as lemon juice, and it improved the dish** (see **Serving** above). The soup does want acid; it wanted it at the bowl, not in the pot.
