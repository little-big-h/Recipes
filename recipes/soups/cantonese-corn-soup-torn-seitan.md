# Cantonese Corn Soup with Torn Seitan [09-11-26] ✴️

*An eighth corn profile, and **the first in the lineage that is not a blended purée**. Built as the vehicle for [`torn-seitan-for-soup.md`](../oven-mains/torn-seitan-for-soup.md): the traditional Cantonese 粟米湯 carries shredded chicken, and the torn seitan takes that place exactly. 🌽 A third of the corn is **charred hard and dry** before anything else happens, which is the one step none of the seven matrix profiles does and the reason this does not taste merely sweet.*

## Method

### 1. Wok — char, then sweat

1. **🌽 400 g of the corn, dry in the wok, hard**, no oil, until the kernels are spotted brown and starting to pop. Tip out and reserve.
   > ⚠⚠ **This is the step that decides whether the soup is one-note.** 1.5 kg of corn plus 390 g of onion is a sweet pot, and Maillard on the kernels is the only thing in the dish that supplies savoury depth in the opposite direction. ⚠ **Frozen kernels mean there are no cobs**, so there is no corn stock to build on either — the body comes from blending and the depth comes from browning. Nothing else can stand in for it.
2. **🧅 onion, 🧄 garlic, 🫚 ginger into the same wok with 🥑 2 g oil** and sweat soft without colour.
   > **2 g here, and 3 g later for the seitan — 5 g of oil in the entire dish.** The charring in step 1 uses none at all, and the aromatics only need enough not to catch.

### 2. Pot — simmer and blend

3. **Deglaze the wok into the Instant Pot** — the charring left fond and that fond is the rest of the savoury depth.
4. **🌽 700 g corn, 🍥 dashi powder and 1800 ml 💧 water into the pot.** Simmer **18 minutes**.
5. **Blend smooth, then stop.**
   > ⚠ **Leave it pourable.** The body has to be light enough that a seitan strand sits in it rather than being coated by it. There is no 🥔 starch in this soup and that is deliberate.
6. **🌽 the remaining 400 g whole corn and the 400 g charred corn go in after the blend.** Simmer 8 minutes.
   > ⚠ **The char only survives if it never meets the blender.** This is the whole reason the corn is split three ways.
7. **🧀 nooch, 🦪 oyster sauce and 🧂 salt in**, then hold off the boil.
   > **Three umami sources that multiply rather than add**: shiitake guanylate in the 🍥 dashi, mushroom guanylate in the 🦪 LKK sauce, and 🧀 glutamate. ⚠ The 🦪 sauce is roughly **30% sugar**, so the 15 g increase over the plan pushes the same direction the charring is pulling against.

### 3. Broccoli — never in the pot

8. **🥦 Microwave-steam the broccoli, covered, with a tablespoon of water**, and pull it **firmly under-done.**
   > ✅ **~1.4% loss** — the lowest figure in the project's table, against ~20% stir-fried from raw. ⚠ **It never goes in the pot.** Simmered, it greys and turns sulfurous; 538 g is a tenth of the bowl, not a garnish, so that would be a real loss.
   > ✅ **Peel and dice the stalk and use it.** It is the crunchiest part of the plant and the texture this was added for.
   > ✅ **No acid in the pot means it stays bright green.** Acid converts chlorophyll to pheophytin — the constraint the Tom Yum Broccoli soup had to design around, and the reason the 🥭 amchur is at the table.
   > ⚠ **Cut the florets to 1.5–2 cm**, smaller than for a side, so a spoonful carries corn, broccoli and seitan together. And expect residual heat in the soup to keep cooking them.

### 4. Seitan and assembly

9. **🥩 Brown the torn seitan hard** in 🥑 3 g oil until the ragged edges catch. The whole 716 g batch goes in.
   > ⚠ Torn surfaces have an enormous area. Searing builds a skin that resists waterlogging, and **skipping it is the main way seitan goes spongy in soup.**
10. **🥩 seitan and 🥦 broccoli into the bowls, soup ladled over.** Not simmered in.
    > ⚠ **Nothing holds the solids in suspension**, since there is no starch — kernels and seitan settle. Stir before each ladle, and again when reheating.
11. **🥭 Amchur at the table.** Stir 10–12 g into a few spoons of the hot soup to make a loose slurry first — dry powder clumps on liquid — and let people spoon it in.

## Design notes

- **Why this is outside the seven-profile matrix.** Every profile in [`../../design/CORN-SOUPS.md`](../../design/CORN-SOUPS.md) is a creamy blended purée built on a 2 kg corn base with a legume cooked pot-in-pot. This one keeps the corn base, drops the legume entirely in favour of the seitan, and **deliberately does not finish as a purée** — a strand needs a liquid to sit in. It is the eighth profile, not a variant of one of the seven.
- **The family record pointed here before the cook did.** 🧒 **Jannes scored 6.0 on the Japanese profile** against a 7.0 prediction — the largest miss in the matrix — and the standing hypothesis is the 18 g of red miso substituted on the day. This profile has no fermented intensity at all. 🧒 **Jannes also hit his 8.0 ceiling on the Tom Yum Broccoli soup**, a brassica he is flagged against (obs 57), and 🧒 **Lara prefers broccoli to asparagus**, stated directly (obs 55).
- **Anja is the rater this design is really aimed at.** Remark 66's working hypothesis is that she rewards savoury, umami-forward and texturally varied dishes and marks down sweet one-note purées. 1.5 kg of corn is a sweet one-note purée by default. The charring, the three umami sources, and **four textures against a smooth base** — whole kernels, charred kernels, seitan strands, broccoli and stalk — are all aimed at the same reading.
- **🥭 Amchur ended at the table rather than in the pot, and the argument moved twice.** Four objections were raised against putting it in: it greys the 🥦 broccoli, it hydrolyses starch and thins the soup, it tightens 🥚 egg ribbons, and it breaks observation 1 — acid off the main pot. **Three of those then dissolved**: the egg was dropped, the starch was dropped, and Holger overruled the Lara constraint. Only the broccoli survived, and since the broccoli is bowl-side anyway it would have been survivable. It is at the table because that is where it ends up being more useful, not because the pot could not take it.
- **Considered and not used:** 🥚 egg ribbons, the traditional Cantonese finish — dropped because ~5 kg is a two-meal pot and egg ribbons go rubbery on reheating; 🥔 potato starch, dropped because the blended corn carries enough body and a bound soup coats the strands; ❓ white pepper, the defining seasoning of this soup and genuinely missed, but not in the kitchen.
