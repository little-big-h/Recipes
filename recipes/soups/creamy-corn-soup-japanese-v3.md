# Creamy Corn Soup — Japanese Profile (v3: homemade kombu-shiitake dashi)

*Builds on v2 (white miso, family default). The change: the 1800 ml of plain water is replaced by a homemade **kombu-shiitake dashi**, and the salty bottled Tsuyu is cut back hard (104 → 45 ml) to let the real dashi carry the savoury backbone. White miso, corn, soybeans, bok choi and all else held at v2 levels. Real hon-mirin replaces the old mirin-style seasoning, dropped to 22 ml because hon-mirin reads sweeter (see `../../docs/TECHNIQUES.md`). Net effect: a deeper, rounder dashi character at roughly **half the salt** (~7 g vs ~11 g). Family of five.*

## Method

> **The night before — make the dashi (mizudashi, cold brew):** Wipe the 🌊 kombu with a damp cloth — do **not** rinse off the white surface bloom (that's mannitol, umami). Submerge it in the 💧 1800 ml cold water along with the 🍄 shiitake powder. Cover and refrigerate overnight. Before cooking, **lift out the kombu** and reserve it (slice into tsukudani with a splash of mirin + soy, or drop a strip in with the soybeans). **Never boil the kombu itself** — only the finished, kombu-free dashi gets heated.
>
> **On the day:** Allow 🌽 corn to partially thaw before loading. No soaking needed for 🫘 soybeans. Shred 🥬 bok choi finely (chiffonade the leaves, fine julienne the stems) during the long pressure cook — set aside.

1. Place rack in Ninja. Put 🫘 soybeans in steaming basket and set on rack. Add 🌽 corn, the **kombu-shiitake dashi** (the 1800 ml brew, 🌊 kombu removed), 🍶 Tsuyu and 🟤 dried ginger powder. Seal — high pressure, 35 min, natural release.

2. At about 100 minutes (during natural release): slice 🌱 spring onion, keeping white and green parts separate. Sauté white parts in 🌻 sesame oil in wok on low heat, 2–3 min until softened. Set aside.

3. At about 123 minutes (Ninja fully released): lift out basket of 🫘 soybeans and set aside. Blend 🌽 corn completely smooth. Adjust consistency with a splash of cooking liquid if needed.

4. Return blended corn to Ninja on low heat. Add 🍯 hon-mirin and bring to a brief simmer for 20–30 sec to cook off the alcohol. Pull back to the lowest heat — **no boiling from here on.** Whisk in 🫗 dashi-soy and 🫙 white miso, tasting as you go: Tsuyu is cut back hard this time so there's more salt headroom than in v1/v2, but dashi-soy and miso are still salty. Stir in sautéed 🌱 spring onion white parts and drained 🫘 soybeans. Finally, fold in shredded 🥬 bok choi — residual heat wilts it in 30–60 seconds; stems retain slight crunch.

5. Serve in bowls. Scatter 🌱 spring onion green parts, ⚫ sesame seeds and 🌿 nori flakes. Drizzle 🌻 sesame oil at the table.

## Experiment design notes

**Hypothesis:** A real kombu-shiitake dashi backbone beats the bottled-concentrate stack — equal or better depth and family ratings — at roughly half the salt. If it lands, this becomes the default build for the Japanese line and a salt-budget win.

**Honest confound (read before attributing results):** this is *not* a clean single-variable change. It bundles two coupled moves:

1. **Umami source:** plain water → kombu-shiitake dashi (adds real glutamate × guanylate depth).
2. **Salt level:** Tsuyu cut 104 → 45 ml (salt ~11 g → ~7 g).

They're coupled on purpose — the whole reason to add real dashi is that it lets you cut the salty concentrate. But it means a ratings change can't be cleanly attributed to *dashi quality* vs *lower salt*. If the family scores it lower, the prime suspect is under-salting, not the dashi. The mirin change (30 → 22 ml hon-mirin) is a third, smaller variable.

**To decouple later (if v3 is ambiguous):** cook a v3b that holds salt at ~11 g (back-fill the cut Tsuyu salt with plain salt or liquid aminos) so the *only* change from v2 is the dashi source. That isolates dashi quality from salt level.

**Predicted outcomes:**

| Outcome | Interpretation |
|:--------|:---------------|
| Family holds or rises vs v2 | Dashi swap validated — adopt as default; salt cut is free |
| Adults hold, kids dip | Likely under-salted for younger palates, not a dashi problem — run v3b at held salt |
| Across-the-board dip | Either under-salted or the dashi character isn't landing — v3b decouples which |
| Holger especially up | Real dashi depth is the adult-palate lever red miso used to provide, without the fermented edge Jannes disliked |

**Vegetarian note:** confirm the Tsuyu and dashi-soy bottles are bonito-free (see `../../docs/PANTRY.md` flag). The homemade kombu-shiitake dashi is guaranteed vegetarian regardless.
