# Potato, Cauliflower & Spinach Curry with Air-Fried Tofu

*A weeknight coconut curry: 🥔 potato and 🥦 cauliflower **air-fried separately** (caramelised edges instead of boiled-soft), folded into an 🧅 onion bhuna finished with 🍛 mild curry powder, a bhuna'd 🍅 tomato purée and 🥥 light coconut milk, with 🥬 spinach wilted in at the end. 🧊 Tofu air-fries as its own side, timed to land hot at the same moment. Mild and family-friendly — no chilli in the pot. Family of five.*

## Method

1. **Toss and start the potato.** Cube the 🥔 potato, toss with ~5 g 🥑 oil. Air-fry **200C, ~20 min**, shaking once, until almost done (not fully soft — it finishes in the sauce).
2. **Jumpstart the onion.** While the potato fries, microwave the 🧅 onion (chopped) ~2-3 min to pre-soften — the standard scorch-avoidance move before it hits the wok.
3. **Bhuna the onion.** Transfer to the wok with a little 🥑 oil; cook until deep golden, ~5-6 min.
4. **Bloom the curry powder.** Add the 🍛 mild curry powder late, once the onion is properly bhuna'd — stir ~30-60 sec in the residual oil to wake up the spices before any liquid goes in.
5. **Bhuna the tomato purée.** Add the 🍅 tomato purée and cook it down ~1 min until it visibly darkens — the umami anchor, same move as the lobia masala's tomato paste. Judge it by colour, not the clock.
6. **Add the coconut milk.** Pour in the 🥥 light coconut milk to deglaze, stir to combine, bring to a gentle simmer — not a rolling boil, or the light (lower-fat) coconut milk can split.
7. **Transfer the potato.** Once the air-fried 🥔 potato is almost done, fold it into the sauce to finish cooking through and pick up flavour.
8. **Air-fry the cauliflower.** Into the now-free air fryer: 🥦 cauliflower, tossed with ~5 g 🥑 oil, **200C, ~20 min**, shaking once, until caramelised at the edges.
9. **Fold in cauliflower and spinach.** Add the air-fried 🥦 cauliflower and the raw 🥬 spinach to the wok together; wilt the spinach through, ~2-3 min.
10. **Tofu, separately.** Cube the 🧊 tofu, toss with ~5 g 🥑 oil, air-fry **200C, ~15 min**, shaking once, until firm and golden — timed to land hot alongside the finished curry.
11. **Serve** — curry with the air-fried tofu on the side. Taste and salt at the table — the pot itself is low-salt by design.

## Design notes

- **Potato and cauliflower air-fried separately, not boiled in the sauce.** Same move as the lobia masala's butternut and the tikka curry's cauliflower — caramelised edges from dry heat, folded in late rather than dissolved into the sauce. Trade-off worth naming: air-frying browns and slightly seals the surface, so it absorbs less of the curry's flavour than a raw potato simmered straight in the sauce would — the choice here favours the caramelised edges and firmer bite over maximum flavour penetration.
- **Onion bhuna is the umami anchor**, same architecture as the coconut-tomato veg & chickpea curry and the lobia masala — microwave-jumpstart to avoid scorching, then a real bhuna in the wok before any liquid goes in.
- **Curry powder added late, after the onion is properly browned** — blooming it in residual oil rather than at the start avoids it scorching before the onion's own sugars have developed.
- **Tomato purée bhuna'd before the coconut milk goes in** — the same umami-anchor move as the lobia masala's tomato paste ("a tomato base goes flat without one"). Cooked down until it visibly darkens, not on a timer.
- **No chilli in the pot** — mild by design, family-friendly. Heat can go at the table if wanted.
- **Tofu genuinely separate, not folded into the sauce** — a plain air-fried side rather than simmered/blended in, so it stays firm and distinct rather than picking up the curry's moisture.
- **Single air-fry batches, not split** — the ML750 has enough basket capacity that potato and cauliflower each go in as one batch, not the cautious two-batch default used elsewhere in the project (hasselback potatoes, the soybean-corn soup) where capacity was never actually confirmed. Worth logging whether this holds on the real cook — it'd resolve an assumption flagged open in a couple of other recipes too.
- **Quantities are a first pass, not yet confirmed** — curry powder (30 g), tomato purée (50 g) and coconut milk (400 ml) are as specified; potato/cauliflower/spinach/onion/tofu are estimated to a family-of-five scale matching the coconut-tomato veg & chickpea curry's proportions. Log actual amounts used in the **Actual used** column and the real cooked yield once done — the `.foodnoms` link will be rebuilt against those numbers rather than this planned estimate.
