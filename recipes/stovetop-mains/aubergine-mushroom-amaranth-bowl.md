# Aubergine, Mushroom & Amaranth Bowl

*A leftovers-forward bowl for two: salted, air-fried **aubergine** and separately air-fried **mushrooms** tossed with reheated **leftover cooked amaranth** and **leftover air-fried tofu**, dressed in a **red miso + capers** sauce (the same umami-acid pairing that made the historical Aubergine · Passata · Amaranth dish score 8.6/10, with red in for white — an adult-only dish, and a good way to use up the red miso jar). An optional pinch of shiitake powder stacks a second umami source on top — glutamate (miso) × guanylate (shiitake) multiply rather than add. Built around amaranth nobody else in the family much likes, so it's a Holger + Anja plate, not a full family dish. Generous for two.*

## Method

> **Salt the aubergine first — the one step that matters.** Aubergine is notoriously oil-thirsty and goes spongy in the air fryer if cooked straight from raw. Salting and resting draws out the bitter moisture; patting dry before oiling is what lets it actually crisp instead of steam.

1. **Cut and salt the 🍆 aubergine.** Cube it, salt generously, spread on a rack or paper towel. Rest **15–20 min**.
2. **Air-fry the 🍄 mushrooms — while the aubergine rests.** Toss with ~3 g 🥑 avocado oil and a pinch of salt. **200 °C, ~9–10 min**, shaking once. The air fryer's otherwise idle during the aubergine's rest, so this costs no extra time. Set aside once done.
3. **Pat dry, toss with oil.** Once rested, pat the 🍆 aubergine thoroughly dry, then toss with ~5 g 🥑 avocado oil.
4. **Air-fry the aubergine.** **200 °C, ~18 min**, shaking once, until well browned. *Same temperature as the mushrooms — high heat is what you want for both: it drives off the mushrooms' surface moisture fast enough to brown rather than stew, and it's what crisps the aubergine's oiled edges.*
5. **Dry-fry the 🌾 amaranth.** Break up the cold amaranth with a spoon first — it'll be a dense block from the fridge. Into a hot, dry (nonstick) pan, **no added liquid**. Let it sit **undisturbed** for a minute or so at a time rather than stirring constantly, so it picks up a light golden crust where it touches the pan, then turn and repeat. **~5 min** total. *This is the fix for sludgy reheated amaranth — it's very starchy, so any added liquid (water, a splash for "gentle" reheating) just resoftens it back into the same gluey texture. Dry heat drives off surface moisture instead of adding more.*
6. **Warm the 🧊 tofu through.** Push the amaranth aside (or set aside), then warm the tofu in the same hot pan, **~2–3 min** — it just needs heat, not the amaranth's technique.
7. **Whisk the 🍶 red miso.** Off heat, in a splash of hot water, until smooth. **Never returns to heat once whisked in** — red miso turns harsh if boiled, more so than white. Stir in the 🍂 shiitake powder here too, if using.
8. **Combine.** Toss the air-fried 🍆 aubergine, 🍄 mushrooms, 🧊 tofu and 🌾 amaranth together, pour over the 🍶 miso (+ 🍂 shiitake) mixture, scatter the 🫒 capers through. Taste — the capers carry the acid this bowl needs; add more to taste.

## Design notes

- **The aubergine-passata-amaranth precedent.** This dish's flavour DNA is lifted straight from the family's historical 8.6/10 dish: aubergine + amaranth + miso + capers. That recipe braises everything for ~100 min; this is the fast version, using leftovers to skip the two slowest components (the grain and the protein are already done).
- **Red miso, not white — adult-only licence.** Red is more assertive and fermented-forward, which is exactly why it's the family default to avoid (Jannes in particular finds it underwhelming/too funky) but works well here since it's a Holger + Anja plate only. Also uses up the red miso jar sitting in stock. Quantity follows the project's standard substitution (30 g white ≈ 18 g red), scaled down from this recipe's original 20 g white to **12 g red**.
- **Salt-rest is the one aubergine-specific step.** Everything else in the household's usual air-fry rotation (cauliflower, squash) goes in raw-tossed; aubergine's high water/oil-absorption means skipping the salt rest here specifically leads to a soggy, oil-heavy result.
- **Mushrooms air-fried separately, not alongside the aubergine.** Mushrooms dump a lot of water as they cook; sharing a basket with the aubergine would steam both rather than crisp either. Running them as their own batch — during the aubergine's otherwise-idle salt-rest window — sidesteps the crowding problem without costing any extra time.
- **Don't lower the heat for mushrooms — they want it hot, not gentle.** The instinct to turn the temperature down (as you would for a scorch-prone dry spice) is backwards here: mushrooms are ~92% water, and at low heat they just release liquid and stew in it rather than brown. High heat evaporates that liquid fast enough for actual Maillard browning to start — the same reason a crowded, lukewarm pan gives soggy mushrooms and a hot one gives seared ones. 200 °C, matching the aubergine, is the right call.
- **Dry-fry the amaranth, don't just reheat it wet.** Amaranth's very starchy and cooks up porridge-like rather than as discrete grains; cooled and reheated with any added moisture (microwave, a splash of water) just resoftens it back into the same sludgy texture. A hot, dry pan — undisturbed for stretches so a crust can form — drives moisture off instead, the same fix that rescues clumpy leftover rice for fried rice.
- **Umami stacking, not just seasoning.** Miso is glutamate; shiitake powder is guanylate — pairing them multiplies rather than adds (the same lever behind the project's kombu-shiitake dashi and the Thai green shakshuka's shiitake fix). Capers supply the acid the family's original 8.6/10 version also leaned on — this bowl needs an acid note or it reads flat against the earthy amaranth and meaty mushroom.
- **Two-person, not five.** Built around amaranth that "nobody other than Holger seems to like," so this is a Holger + Anja plate rather than a full family dish — no need to design around Lara/Jannes/Julina preferences here.
- **Pantry fit.** Miso, capers, shiitake powder and oil are all standing pantry stock — only the aubergine and mushrooms need buying, and both are plain fresh produce, not spices/pastes, so this doesn't cut against the pantry run-down.
