# Tom Yum Butternut & Cauliflower Curry

*A creamy-but-coconut-light curry built on three sweet, round elements — roasted 🎃 butternut, 🥥 light coconut milk and 🍮 silken tofu, blended smooth — with 🍲 **Mae Ploy Vegan Tom Yum Paste** as the sharp counterweight. Roasted 🥦 cauliflower goes in at the last second for texture, 🥬 spinach for colour. **The vegan jar is the right one and the only one to use here** — the standard Mae Ploy tom yum paste contains shrimp paste, fish sauce and dried shrimp (see Design notes). Heat and acid stay off the pot and go on the table, so the pot itself is mild, sweet and smooth — Jannes's register — while the adults sharpen their own bowls. Serves 5.*

## Method

> **Two rules govern this dish.** 🍮 **Silken tofu goes in off the heat** — boiling it turns it grainy — and once it is in, the pan never returns to more than a bare simmer. And **the 🥦 cauliflower does not sit in the sauce**: texture is its entire job, and it softens within minutes of going in.

1. **Prep.** Cube the 🎃 butternut squash, skin on, into ~3 cm pieces. Break the 🥦 cauliflower into large florets — larger than you think, they shrink. Dice the 🧅 onion.
2. **Roast the squash.** Toss the 🎃 squash with about half the 🥑 oil, spread on a tray in a single layer, and roast at 200 C for about 35 minutes, until the edges catch and a piece collapses under a spoon. At 798 g this should fit a single tray comfortably — but **spread it out properly**, because crowded squash steams instead of roasting and the roast is where the sweetness comes from.
3. **Air-fry the cauliflower — split it across two appliances if the basket won't take it in one layer.** Toss the 🥦 florets with a little more 🥑 oil, then air-fry at 200 C for about 20 minutes, shaking once, until browned at the edges. ⚠ **A crowded basket steams instead of browning, which destroys the only reason the cauliflower is in this dish.** At the as-cooked 931 g that is well over one basket: put roughly a third on a second tray in the oven alongside the 🎃 squash from the start (it is already at 200 C) and air-fry the rest, so both finish in the same window with no batching and nothing sitting around going soft. The oven-roasted share will be a little less crisp than the air-fried — put that share in first at the table, or fold it into the sauce and keep the crisper air-fried pieces on top.
4. **Fry the onion.** In the remaining 🥑 oil over medium heat, cook the 🧅 onion until soft and just starting to colour, about 4 minutes.
5. **Fry the paste.** Add the 🍲 tom yum paste and fry for 3–4 minutes until it darkens a shade and smells cooked rather than raw. **This step is not optional** — paste stirred into liquid stays fragrant on top and hollow underneath.
6. **Build the base.** Add the roasted 🎃 squash, the drained 🫘 butter beans, the 🥥 coconut milk, the 🍃 makrut lime leaves and about 150 ml 💧 water. **Start dry and add water only if it will not blend** — the squash is the body of this base, and at 798 g there is less of it than a full batch, so over-thinning is the easy mistake. Bring to a **gentle** simmer — never a hard boil, which splits coconut milk — and hold for 5–6 minutes so the leaves give up their oils and the 🫘 beans warm through and take on the paste.
7. **Blend.** Fish out the 🍃 lime leaves and discard them. **Take the pan off the heat**, add the 🍮 silken tofu, and blend the lot — 🫘 beans included — until completely smooth — no visible curds, no texture. Blend in batches or with a stick blender; a thoroughly smooth base is the point, not a nearly smooth one.
8. **Finish off the heat.** Return the base to a low heat just to warm through. Stir in the 🍄 seven-mushroom powder **off the heat**, then wilt the 🥬 spinach through for 1–2 minutes. **Taste before adding any salt at all** — the 🍲 paste has already put ~5.6 g in the pot.
9. **Fold and serve.** Fold the roasted 🥦 cauliflower through at the very last moment, or spoon it over each bowl. Serve with 🍋 lime wedges and crushed 🌶️ chilli **on the table, not in the pot** — the chilli stays off the pot regardless of who is eating, for Jannes.

## Design notes

- **Use the *vegan* Mae Ploy jar — and check which one you have, because both exist.** Mae Ploy sells two tom yum pastes and they are not interchangeable. The **standard** one lists shrimp paste 2%, fish sauce (anchovy) 2% and dried shrimp 1%. The **Vegan** one (orange tub, VEGAN badge, "Vegetarian Tom Yum paste", 400 g, barcode `8850367994076`) has none of that: **lemongrass 38.2%, galangal 9.6%, water 8.0%, shallot 6.9%, tamarind 6.4%, citric acid 5.3%, soybean oil 5.0%, salt 4.6%, garlic 4.2%, sugar 3.0%, fermented soybean paste 1.5%, hydrolyzed soy protein 1.2%, kaffir lime peel 0.4%.** Contains soy; no MSG, no preservative, gluten free. This recipe assumes the vegan jar. *(Recorded in `docs/PANTRY.md` so this doesn't get re-litigated — it has come up more than once.)*
- **The vegan jar already solves the umami problem the right way.** Removing shrimp paste and fish sauce removes glutamate, and simply omitting it is the 2026-06-06 flat-Indian failure — nothing in the soy's place, dish goes flat, salt gets chased. Mae Ploy's vegan version substitutes **fermented soybean paste + hydrolyzed soy protein**, which is glutamate-for-glutamate. That is why no aminos are needed here. The 6 g of 🍄 seven-mushroom powder is still worth adding: it contributes **guanylate**, and glutamate × guanylate multiplies.
- **It also covers the tamarind shortage.** The jar is 6.4% tamarind, so the sour-fruity note that's currently out of stock arrives inside the paste.
- **Salt is the binding constraint, not flavour.** The label reads **13.9 g salt per 100 g**, so 40 g puts ~5.6 g in the pot before any other ingredient — hence no added salt anywhere in this recipe. ⚠ Note the label doesn't reconcile internally: the ingredient list declares salt at 4.6%, which with the fermented soy and HVP would suggest ~5 g/100 g, not 13.9. The nutrition panel is the **conservative** figure and is what's used here, but treat 13.9 as an upper bound and taste rather than trusting it.
- **The dish is three sweet things and one sharp one.** Roasted 🎃 squash, 🥥 coconut and blended 🍮 tofu are all round and sweet-to-neutral; without a counterweight this is baby food. The tom yum is that counterweight, and it is structural rather than decorative — the same role sumac plays in the Levantine bhuna shakshuka. If the finished pot tastes flat and sweet, the answer is more lime at the table, not more salt.
- **Coconut plus tom yum is not a fusion liberty.** *Tom kha* and tom yum *nam khon* are both creamy tom yums, so the coconut base is squarely in-style. What is deliberately absent is added sugar — the jar is already 3% sugar and the roasted squash has the rest covered several times over.
- **Heat and acid off the pot, by design.** Tom yum's two defining traits are sourness and chilli heat, and those are precisely Lara's flagged dislike (no in-pot acid, dislikes sourness) and Jannes's (heat-sensitive). Rather than water the dish down for everyone, both go on the table — which is also *correct* tom yum technique, since lime's aromatics are volatile and die in a simmer. The pot itself lands mild, sweet, creamy and completely smooth, which is Jannes's register almost exactly. The jar's own dried red chilli is 5.7%, mild at 40 g across 3.2 kg.
- **Why the cauliflower is air-fried and added last.** Blended bases are texturally monotonous, and this one has three smooth components. The cauliflower is the only chew in the dish, so it gets the hottest, driest treatment available and goes in at the last possible moment. Folded in early it is soft within five minutes and the dish loses its only contrast. *(Cauliflower was once flagged as a Jannes dislike, but he scored his best pinto result on a cook that had it steamed on top — see `docs/RATINGS.md` remark 37 — so roasted and browned should be safe.)*
- **🫘 Butter beans go in blended, not whole — they are a body fix, not a texture one.** Added mid-cook (2026-08-04) once the squash came in short at 798 g. Blended they do exactly what the missing squash would have: starchy, creamy volume without reaching for more coconut fat, plus ~15 g of protein and 16 g of fibre at a salt cost of under 0.2 g (the Eat Wholesome tin is no-added-salt, 0.08 g/100 g). Left whole they would have added a *third* texture — soft and lumpy — which blunts rather than reinforces the one contrast this dish is built on: a completely smooth base against crisp cauliflower. If more substance is wanted, hold back ~80 g whole rather than leaving the tin unblended; that reads as deliberate garnish instead of muddle.
- **🍈 Yuzu was considered for the pot and dropped (2026-08-04).** With Lara out, the no-in-pot-acid rule lifted and 80 ml of yuzu was costed in before being cut. Worth recording *why*, because the reasoning survives the decision: tom yum's sour is canonically *manao* lime — blunt and sharp — whereas yuzu is the **weaker acid** (~4–5% citric vs lime's ~6%) and the **stronger aromatic**, so it rounds and perfumes rather than sharpens. Against a "make it more sour" brief that is the wrong instrument, even though it would have reinforced the makrut register the jar's kaffir lime peel and the 🍃 leaves already set up. It also uses most of a small bottle at this pot size. **If in-pot acid is wanted on a future Lara-absent cook, use lime for the sour volume and save yuzu as a finishing note.**
- **Skin-on squash.** It blends out completely once roasted to collapse, and it saves the ~12% peeling loss measured on the sweet-potato soup.
- **Run-down status:** tom yum paste, makrut lime leaves, seven-mushroom blend and a share of the onion glut. Nothing here requires a purchase.
- **If you only have the non-vegan jar**, don't use it — rebuild the aromatic line from stock instead: ~25 g lemongrass paste, ~15 g galangal paste, ~5 g garlic powder and ~30 g coconut aminos for the glutamate, plus ~4 g salt. At the scaled quantities that would be ~17 g lemongrass paste, ~10 g galangal paste, ~3 g garlic powder, ~20 g coconut aminos and ~3 g salt.
