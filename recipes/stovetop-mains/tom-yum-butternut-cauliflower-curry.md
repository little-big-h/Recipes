# Tom Yum Butternut & Cauliflower Curry

*A creamy-but-coconut-light curry built on three sweet, round elements — roasted 🎃 butternut, 🥥 light coconut milk and 🍮 silken tofu, blended smooth — with a **vegetarian tom yum** aromatic line as the sharp counterweight. Roasted 🥦 cauliflower goes in at the last second for texture, 🥬 spinach for colour. **The tom yum is built from stocked aromatics rather than a jar: commercial Mae Ploy tom yum paste contains shrimp paste, fish sauce and dried shrimp** (see Design notes). Heat and acid stay off the pot and go on the table, so the pot itself is mild, sweet and smooth — Jannes's register — while the adults sharpen their own bowls. Serves 5 with leftovers.*

## Method

> **Two rules govern this dish.** 🍮 **Silken tofu goes in off the heat** — boiling it turns it grainy — and once it is in, the pan never returns to more than a bare simmer. And **the 🥦 cauliflower does not sit in the sauce**: texture is its entire job, and it softens within minutes of going in.

1. **Prep.** Cube the 🎃 butternut squash, skin on, into ~3 cm pieces. Break the 🥦 cauliflower into large florets — larger than you think, they shrink. Dice the 🧅 onion.
2. **Roast the squash.** Toss the 🎃 squash with about half the 🥑 oil, spread on a tray in a single layer, and roast at 200 C for about 35 minutes, until the edges catch and a piece collapses under a spoon. **Two trays if it does not fit in one layer** — crowded squash steams instead of roasting, and the roast is where the sweetness comes from.
3. **Air-fry the cauliflower.** Toss the 🥦 florets with a little more 🥑 oil, then air-fry at 200 C for about 20 minutes, shaking once, until browned at the edges. The oven works too, but the fryer runs hotter and drier and frees the oven for the squash.
4. **Fry the onion.** In the remaining 🥑 oil over medium heat, cook the 🧅 onion until soft and just starting to colour, about 4 minutes.
5. **Crack the aromatics.** Add the 🌾 lemongrass paste, 🫚 galangal paste and 🧄 garlic powder and fry for 3–4 minutes until they darken a shade and smell cooked rather than raw. **This step is not optional** — pastes stirred into liquid stay fragrant on top and hollow underneath.
6. **Build the base.** Add the roasted 🎃 squash, the 🥥 coconut milk, the 🍃 makrut lime leaves and about 300 ml 💧 water. Bring to a **gentle** simmer — never a hard boil, which splits coconut milk — and hold for 5–6 minutes so the leaves give up their oils.
7. **Blend.** Fish out the 🍃 lime leaves and discard them. **Take the pan off the heat**, add the 🍮 silken tofu, and blend until completely smooth — no visible curds, no texture. Blend in batches or with a stick blender; a thoroughly smooth base is the point, not a nearly smooth one.
8. **Finish off the heat.** Return the base to a low heat just to warm through. Stir in the 🍄 seven-mushroom powder and 🫗 coconut aminos **off the heat**, then wilt the 🥬 spinach through for 1–2 minutes. **Taste before adding any 🧂 salt** — the aminos have already gone in.
9. **Fold and serve.** Fold the roasted 🥦 cauliflower through at the very last moment, or spoon it over each bowl. Serve with 🍋 lime wedges and crushed 🌶️ chilli **on the table, not in the pot**.

## Design notes

- **⚠ Commercial tom yum paste is not vegetarian — that is why this build does not use it.** [Mae Ploy Tom Yum paste](https://mae-ploy.com/mae-ploy-tom-yum-paste/) lists **shrimp paste 2%, fish sauce (anchovy) 2% and dried shrimp 1%** alongside the lemongrass and galangal. Most commercial tom yum pastes are the same — the shrimp-and-fish backbone is part of what the style *is*. The same caution already applies to bottled tsuyu in `docs/PANTRY.md`. So the aromatic line here is rebuilt from stock instead, which turns out to cost very little: lemongrass is 22% of the Mae Ploy jar and galangal another 5%, and both are stocked as pastes.
- **Replacing the shrimp and fish sauce, not just omitting them.** What those ingredients supply is *glutamate plus a marine savoury depth*, and dropping them without a substitute is the 2026-06-06 flat-Indian failure — nothing in the soy's place, dish goes flat, salt gets chased. Here 🫗 coconut aminos carry the glutamate and 🍄 seven-mushroom powder the **guanylate**, and the two multiply. Mae Ploy also lists HVP and added MSG, both pure glutamate, so this is a like-for-like replacement of the mechanism rather than an approximation of the flavour.
- **The dish is three sweet things and one sharp one.** Roasted 🎃 squash, 🥥 coconut and blended 🍮 tofu are all round and sweet-to-neutral; without a counterweight this is baby food. The tom yum aromatic line is that counterweight, and it is structural rather than decorative — the same role sumac plays in the Levantine bhuna shakshuka. If the finished pot tastes flat and sweet, the answer is more lemongrass and lime, not more salt.
- **Coconut plus tom yum is not a fusion liberty.** *Tom kha* and tom yum *nam khon* are both creamy tom yums, so the coconut base is squarely in-style. What is deliberately absent is the sugar — Mae Ploy's jar is 9% sugar, and the roasted squash has that covered several times over.
- **Heat and acid off the pot, by design.** Tom yum's two defining traits are sourness and chilli heat, and those are precisely Lara's flagged dislike (no in-pot acid, dislikes sourness) and Jannes's (heat-sensitive). Rather than water the dish down for everyone, both go on the table — which is also *correct* tom yum technique, since lime's aromatics are volatile and die in a simmer. The pot itself lands mild, sweet, creamy and completely smooth, which is Jannes's register almost exactly.
- **Why the cauliflower is air-fried and added last.** Blended bases are texturally monotonous, and this one has three smooth components. The cauliflower is the only chew in the dish, so it gets the hottest, driest treatment available and goes in at the last possible moment. Folded in early it is soft within five minutes and the dish loses its only contrast. *(Cauliflower was once flagged as a Jannes dislike, but he scored his best pinto result on a cook that had it steamed on top — see `docs/RATINGS.md` remark 37 — so roasted and browned should be safe.)*
- **Skin-on squash.** It blends out completely once roasted to collapse, and it saves the ~12% peeling loss measured on the sweet-potato soup.
- **Run-down status:** lemongrass paste, galangal paste, makrut lime leaves, garlic powder, seven-mushroom blend, coconut aminos and a share of the onion glut. Nothing here requires a purchase.
- **If you do use a jar** (accepting the shrimp/fish content): ~40 g of Mae Ploy replaces the lemongrass, galangal, garlic powder and aminos entirely. It is ~10.1 g salt per 100 g, so 40 g puts ~4 g of salt in the pot on its own — drop the added 🧂 salt to zero and taste before adding any.
