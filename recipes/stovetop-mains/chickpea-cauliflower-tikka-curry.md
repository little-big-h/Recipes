# Chickpea, Cauliflower & Spinach Tikka Curry (Kids' Pasta Bowl)

*A mild, tomato-rich tikka curry built in the Ninja: unsoaked chickpeas pressure-cooked in plain water (no tomato — acid stops beans softening), then passata, cauliflower, whole-wheat penne and spinach simmered into a one-pot pasta-curry. Cooked **for the kids** (Holger not eating) — pasta-forward and gentle on purpose, a deliberately safe landing after the soy-bean soup. No soak, no draining.*

## Method

> **No soak.** Unsoaked chickpeas go straight in — they get the full pressure cook.

1. **Bloom.** Melt the 🧈 ghee in the Ninja on sauté. Add the 🌶️ tikka masala powder and bloom ~30 sec until fragrant — ground spices scorch fast, so keep it moving and have the next thing to hand.
2. **Pressure-cook the chickpeas.** Add the 🫘 chickpeas and 💧 water — **no 🍅 passata yet** (tomato acid stops chickpeas softening). Seal — **high pressure, 40 min, natural release** (unsoaked chickpeas; 40 min is the upper end of Nussinow's range, for fully tender). Don't drain.
3. **Reserve a scoop.** Lift out ~3–4 tbsp of 🫘 chickpeas with a little liquid and set aside — a thickening reserve for step 6.
4. **Build the sauce.** On sauté at **medium** (not max sear), stir the 🍅 passata (a full 1000 g — saucy on purpose, to coat the heavy 324 g pasta load) into the chickpeas and their liquid and bring to a simmer. Keep it loose and soupy — the pasta needs that to cook — and **stir**: thick tomato over the base scorches.
5. **Cauliflower, then pasta.** Add the 🥦 cauliflower and simmer ~5 min head-start (the tomato acid holds it firm — which here helps it keep its shape). Stir in the 🌀 penne and simmer **~13–15 min, stirring often**, until pasta and cauliflower are both tender and it's a clinging sauce. With 324 g of penne it'll drink a lot of liquid — splash in water if it tightens before the pasta is done.
6. **Thicken if needed.** If it's still thin, mash the reserved 🫘 chickpeas and stir them back in; otherwise just tip them in whole.
7. **Finish.** Stir the 🥬 spinach through for the last 2–3 min to wilt. Serve (over rice/pasta is already built in).

## Design notes

- **No tomato in the pressure cook.** Acid stabilises pectin and stops chickpeas (like all beans) from softening — so the chickpeas cook in plain water, and the 🍅 passata goes in *after* the release. Same rule that bit the soy beans.
- **Cauliflower simmered in the sauce, not pressure-cooked.** Under pressure it turns to mush (0–3 min is enough to overcook it). Simmered in the open sauce it holds shape — and the tomato acid slowing it is a *feature* here, keeping the florets intact. Hence the ~5-min head start before the pasta.
- **One-pot pasta — whole-wheat, deliberately.** The penne cooks in the curry and its starch thickens the sauce (no cornflour needed). Whole-wheat *gluten* holds the shape; brown-rice or legume pasta would shed starch and go gluey, so they'd need boiling separately. Keep the sauce loose while it cooks, and stir (scorch window).
- **Heavy pasta load → 1000 g passata.** At 324 g dry, the penne is the dominant carb and drinks a lot of liquid (~2× its weight). The passata went up to a full litre to keep enough saucy tomato to coat it — saucier than a typical curry, on purpose for a kids' pasta bowl. With this much pasta starch in play the sauce will thicken itself as it reduces; the chickpea-mash reserve is a backup, not the main thickener here.
- **Thickening = the chickpeas, not cornflour.** Reserve a scoop and mash it back if the sauce is thin — free, on-theme, and avoids a gluey cornflour finish.
- **Built for the kids.** Tikka is *mild*, so heat is no issue for Jannes/Anja; spinach wilted into a tomato sauce slides past kids. **🥦 Cauliflower is Jannes's flagged dislike** — he tolerated it in the bold pinto v2, and mild-tomato-coated it usually passes, but keep florets chunky so he can pick around them if needed.
