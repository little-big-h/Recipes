# Ras el Hanout Mushroom & Tomato Sauce

*An adult-only sauce: **button mushrooms dry-air-roasted** (flat-sliced, no oil, high heat) then simmered into a **ras-el-hanout-bloomed tomato sauce**, finished off heat with **red miso** and **capers**. Served with tofu and barley on the side and **pickled onions** scattered over — mostly held back rather than simmered in, so they stay sharp. Built with **no chilli in the pot** (Anja's heat-sensitive too); chipotle paste (and harissa, if wanted) go **at the table** instead. Same architecture as `ras-el-hanout-spelt-butternut-beans.md` — spice bloomed into tomato, miso off heat, heat held at the table — just faster, and red in for white since this is Holger + Anja only.*

## Method

> **No oil on the mushrooms.** They're going into a soup-like sauce, not eaten crisp on their own — plain dry heat drives off their water and browns them just as well, and skips a step. See Design notes for why oil isn't doing much for browning here anyway.

1. **Slice the 🍄 mushrooms flat.** ~4–6 mm slices, not quarters — more surface area means more of each piece actually browns rather than steaming on the inside. Especially matters if using grill mode: a flat slice lies flush against the element, a quarter only touches at a few points.
2. **Dry-air-roast the mushrooms.** No oil, no salt needed yet. **High heat** — 220–240 °C air-fry, or grill mode if your unit has it (expect to flip partway on grill, since it's mostly one-directional radiant heat). **~10–12 min**, checking more often than you would at 200 °C — thin slices swing from browned to dried out fast at this heat. Some char on the edges is good here, not a flaw; it's headed into a sauce.
3. **Bloom the spice.** In the wok, 🧄 garlic powder + 🍛 ras el hanout in the 🥑 oil, ~30 sec — ras el hanout is warm-spiced, not chilli-hot, so it doesn't reintroduce the heat you're holding back.
4. **Simmer the sauce.** Add the 🥫 canned tomatoes, breaking up any large pieces; simmer **~10 min** while the mushrooms roast alongside.
5. **Combine.** Fold the roasted 🍄 mushrooms into the sauce; simmer together **~5 min** to marry.
6. **Finish off heat.** Whisk in the 🍶 red miso (never returns to heat — more heat-sensitive than white, turns harsh if boiled). Stir through the 🫒 capers.
7. **Serve.** Over/alongside the 🧊 tofu and 🌾 barley (reheated separately). 🪻 Pickled onions scattered on top, mostly held back rather than stirred in, so they stay sharp. 🌶️ Chipotle paste at the table for whoever wants heat.

## Design notes

- **Same architecture as the ras-el-hanout spelt/bean dish, faster.** `ras-el-hanout-spelt-butternut-beans.md` already proved this exact move for the household: spice bloomed into tomato, miso off heat, harissa + lemon held **at the table** to keep heat and acid separate from the pot. This recipe reuses that structure — swap red miso for white (adult-only licence, and using up the red miso jar) and swap the harissa-at-table for chipotle, since that's what's on hand today.
- **Ras el hanout over harissa, tikka, or Thai paste.** Ras el hanout is warm-spiced (cinnamon, cumin, coriander, ginger) rather than chilli-forward, so it doesn't reintroduce the heat being deliberately excluded from the pot. Harissa's entire identity is chilli heat — wrong tool today. Yellow Thai paste carries its own heat and pulls the flavour world (galangal, coconut) away from the capers/pickled-onion/tomato components already chosen.
- **No oil on the mushrooms.** Browning is gated by evaporating the mushroom's own water first, not by oil — oil mostly helps with non-stick and staying supple for standalone eating. Since these are headed into a simmered sauce (which re-adds moisture and softens them further anyway), oil's doing little useful work here. Flat slices instead of quarters compensate by maximising surface area for faster, more even browning.
- **Pickled onions held back, not simmered in.** Simmering would soften and mute them; scattering them on mostly-raw at the end keeps the sharp, vinegary contrast against the deep, warm-spiced sauce — the same logic as keeping capers added late rather than cooked down.
- **Sauce scoped separately from tofu/barley.** Unlike the aubergine-mushroom-amaranth bowl (which combines everything into one tossed dish), this one is a sauce spooned over sides prepared independently — so the nutrition table only covers what's actually costed here.
- **Tomatoes verified, not guessed.** The first pass of this recipe defaulted to a reused generic canned-tomato figure rather than actually querying BWFO for the real product — a shortcut, not a real lookup. A live GraphQL query (`docs/BWFO_GRAPHQL.md`) found the exact match: 800 g is precisely two of BWFO's 400 g Organic Chopped Tomatoes tins, and the numbers already in use turned out to be correct. Worth querying the real product up front next time rather than assuming a generic block is close enough.
