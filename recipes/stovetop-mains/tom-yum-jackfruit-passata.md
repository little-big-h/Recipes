# Tom Yum Pulled Jackfruit in Passata

*A pulled-jackfruit ragù with a Thai spine. 🍈 Young green jackfruit is rinsed, shredded and **dry-fried until it stops steaming and starts browning**, then braised in 🍅 passata sharpened with 🍲 **Mae Ploy Vegan Tom Yum Paste** and the last 10 g of the 🍛 yellow curry jar. 🥥 Light coconut milk and a splash of 🍶 hon-mirin round the two acids off; crisp air-fried 🧊 firm tofu carries all the protein and goes in at the last second. Served over 🍚 rice. **Tomatoes are canonical in tom yum**, so this is a shorter step from Bangkok than it looks. Serves 5.*

## Method

> **Two rules govern this dish.** 🍈 **Jackfruit must be dried before it is braised** — it comes out of the tin waterlogged and slightly sour with brine, and if it goes straight into the 🍅 passata the sauce turns thin and tastes of the can. Rinse it, squeeze it, dry-fry it. And **the 🧊 tofu is folded in at the end, not braised** — it takes twenty minutes in the air fryer to get an edge on it and about four minutes in the sauce to lose it.

1. **Prep.** Drain the 🍈 jackfruit and **rinse it properly under cold water** — a minute in a colander, not a swirl. Cut out the hard core wedge from each piece (or slice it thin; it never softens), then pull the rest apart with your fingers or two forks into shreds. **Squeeze the shreds dry in a clean tea towel.** Cube the 🧊 tofu to ~2 cm. Slice the 🧅 onion.
2. **Air-fry the tofu.** Spray the 🧊 tofu cubes with about half the 🥑 oil and air-fry at 200 C for ~20 minutes, shaking once, until the edges are golden and firm. ⚠ **A crowded basket steams instead of crisping** — do two batches rather than one deep layer. Set aside; it can sit.
3. **Fry the onion.** In the remaining 🥑 oil over medium heat, cook the 🧅 onion for about 8 minutes — past soft, to the point where the edges are properly browning. This is the dish's only sweetness besides the 🍶 mirin, and it has two acids to stand up to.
4. **Dry-fry the jackfruit.** Add the shredded 🍈 jackfruit to the same pan and fry over a decent heat for 5–6 minutes, spreading it out and leaving it alone between stirs, until it stops releasing steam and starts catching colour. **Do not rush this** — it is the step that turns tinned jackfruit into something worth eating.
5. **Fry the pastes.** Push everything aside, add the 🍲 tom yum paste and the 🍛 yellow curry paste to the bare pan, and fry for 3–4 minutes until they darken a shade and smell cooked rather than raw. Stir in the 🧄 garlic powder for the last 30 seconds — no longer, it scorches. **This step is not optional**: paste stirred into liquid stays fragrant on top and hollow underneath.
6. **Braise.** Add the 🍅 passata, the 🥥 coconut milk, the 🍃 makrut lime leaves and about 150 ml 💧 water. Bring to a **gentle** simmer — never a hard boil, which splits coconut milk — and hold for about 22 minutes, stirring occasionally, until it has reduced to a thick ragù that holds a spoon-track. Stir in the 🍶 hon-mirin at about the 15-minute mark so the alcohol cooks off with time to spare.
7. **Cook the rice.** Once the braise is settled, bring the 🍚 rice and 🫗 700 ml water to a boil in a separate pot, then simmer covered for 12 minutes and rest it off the heat, lid on, for 10 more.
8. **Finish off the heat.** Fish out the 🍃 lime leaves and discard. Take the pan off the heat, stir in the 🍄 shiitake powder, then wilt the 🥬 spinach through for 1–2 minutes. **Taste before adding any salt at all** — the two pastes and the 🍈 jackfruit brine have already put ~8 g in the pot.
9. **Fold and serve.** Fold the air-fried 🧊 tofu through at the very last moment, or spoon it over each bowl. Serve over the 🍚 rice, with 🍋 lime wedges and crushed 🌶️ chilli **on the table, not in the pot**.

## Design notes

- **Tomato in tom yum is not a fusion liberty.** *Tom yum nam sai* routinely carries tomato wedges alongside the lemongrass, galangal and lime leaf, so 🍅 passata is a concentration of something already in the dish rather than an import. What is new here is the *quantity* — 500 g of passata makes tomato the base rather than a garnish, which pushes the result toward a Thai-spiced ragù. That is the intended landing spot, and it is why it goes over 🍚 rice rather than into a bowl of broth.
- **🍈 Jackfruit is a texture ingredient, not a protein.** At ~1.2 g per 100 g, the whole 240 g tin contributes about **3 g of protein** — it is a starch-and-fibre food that happens to shred like meat. Treating it as the protein is the standard way to build an under-fuelled vegan main; here the 400 g of 🧊 tofu does that job (69 g) and the jackfruit is left to do the one thing it is genuinely good at.
- **The dry-fry is the whole technique.** Tinned young jackfruit is >90% water and arrives faintly sour from the brine. Braised wet, it dilutes the sauce and keeps the can note. Rinsed, squeezed and dry-fried until it browns, it goes fibrous and absorbent and then drinks up the pot over 22 minutes. Skipping step 4 is the single fastest way to make this dish mediocre. The hard core wedges are the other trap — they stay crunchy however long you cook them, so cut them out or slice them paper-thin.
- **🍛 10 g of yellow curry paste is a jar-finishing move, and worth being honest about what it buys.** At 10 g in a ~3 kg pot it is not a flavour driver — it is turmeric colour and a warm cumin-coriander floor under the lemongrass. It is in the recipe because the jar has 10 g left in it and this is the dish that can absorb them without arguing with the 🍲 tom yum. If the jar turns out to hold 30 g rather than 10, use it all; the profile shifts toward yellow curry and that is a fine dish too, just a different one.
- **Two acids in one pot is the real risk, and it is a Lara risk.** 🍅 Passata is acidic and the 🍲 paste is 5.3% citric acid plus 6.4% tamarind — this is the most acid-forward pot in the tom yum line, and Lara's standing flag is no in-pot acid. Three counterweights are built in and all three are load-bearing: **hard-browned 🧅 onion** (400 g, taken past soft), **🥥 coconut fat**, and **20 ml of 🍶 hon-mirin**, which is the stocked stand-in for the palm sugar this would use in Thailand. `docs/RATINGS.md` observation 54 is the reason to try it anyway: Lara scored par on the Tom Yum Butternut Curry, which carried ~2.1 g of built-in citric acid, suggesting her constraint is *perceived* sourness rather than acid per se. This dish is the harder test of that reading. **If it fails, the fix is more mirin and a longer reduction, not less paste.**
- **Heat stays off the pot** for Jannes, as always — the jar's own dried chilli at 35 g across 3 kg is mild, and 🌶️ crushed chilli plus 🍋 lime go on the table for the adults.
- **Why the wok and not the Ninja.** Pressure would destroy the point: jackfruit shreds go to mush in minutes under pressure, and a sealed pot cannot reduce. This dish wants an open 22-minute simmer that both concentrates the passata and lets the shreds take on the sauce. The Ninja still earns its place as the air fryer for the 🧊 tofu.
- **🍄 Shiitake powder off the heat, for the same reason as always.** The vegan 🍲 jar gets its glutamate from fermented soybean paste and hydrolyzed soy protein; 5 g of dried mushroom brings **guanylate**, and the two multiply. It is the salt-free way to add savour to a pot that already has 8.8 g of salt in it.
- **Substitutions, all in stock.** No 🥥 coconut milk: use 200 ml of the M&S soya drink, but add it **off the heat at the end** — soy protein flocculates near pH 4.5 and this is the most acidic pot in the line (see the Tom Yum Sweet Potato soup for the full treatment). No 🍶 hon-mirin: 2 chopped Medjool dates, simmered in from the start, do the same rounding job. No 🥬 spinach: nothing, it is colour and iron rather than structure.
- **Run-down status: this is a clearing dish.** It empties the 🍛 yellow curry jar, takes 35 g off the 🍲 tom yum glut, uses 400 g of the 🧅 onion surplus, and spends the 🍅 passata and the 🍈 jackfruit tin outright. Nothing here requires a purchase.
