# Buckwheat & Mushroom Patties [22-11-26] ✴️

*A fried patty built on kasha and pulled king oyster, bound by three things at once and held together mostly by what the grain does when it cools. **Designed as the mushroom dish Lara might actually eat** — her objection is to visible mushroom pieces, and a patty is the one format where mushroom reads as texture rather than as identifiable pieces. No added binder beyond what is in the list; no egg.*

> 🍴 **DESIGNED 2026-11-22, NOT YET COOKED.** Quantities are planned. The **Actual used** column is empty until the cook.

## Before you start

**Four things, and the first two are the ones that make or break it.**

1. ⚠⚠ **The mushrooms must be dry before they meet the mix.** A patty full of wet mushroom steams itself apart in the pan, and the free water interferes with both the gluten network and the starch set. 🍄 **King oyster is the driest of the three mushrooms this kitchen uses** — 88.1% water against shiitake’s 89.7% and portobello’s 92.8% — so there is less water to drive off here than the portobello version would have needed. **That is not a reason to shorten the dry phase.** Shredded strands carry several times the surface of dice per gram, so they go from matte to scorched quickly: use the wok method in [`../../docs/TECHNIQUES.md`](../../docs/TECHNIQUES.md), at **lower heat than for diced mushroom**, and judge it by the surfaces rather than by the clock.
2. ⚠⚠ **Mix minimally once the 💪 gluten is in.** Vital wheat gluten hydrates and forms strands when worked, and the torn-seitan file records what that means at concentration: *at 100% it is a rubber band*. Here it is **5.7% of the mix**, which is a binder. **Work it and you get a bouncy puck**, which is the characteristic failure of a cheap veggie burger. This is the project's third gluten application with a third target — the sausage wants it kneaded hard, the torn seitan wants it aligned, this wants it barely developed.
3. ⚠ **Chill the formed patties before frying, overnight if you can.** The buckwheat's starch retrogrades as it cools and the patties firm up considerably; cold-firmness sits on top of that. It is the cheapest thing you can do for structural success and it costs only clock time.
4. ⚠ **No liquid smoke.** It was in the brief and it is out, because **Lara is flagged against smoke** and it is the one ingredient here that cannot be moved to the table — it would be inside the patty. Put a smoky sauce beside them instead.

## Method

### 1. Grain

1. 🌾 **Cook the 200 g of kasha** in ~350 ml water, then **spread it out to cool.** Spreading matters twice over: it cools fast enough to be safe, and the retrogradation that firms it starts sooner.
   > ⚠ **Check whether your buckwheat is kasha or raw groats.** If raw, **dry-toast it before cooking** — that toasting is where most of the nutty flavour comes from, and it is the main thing the grain contributes besides bulk.

### 2. Wok — three things in sequence, nothing washed between

2. 🫘 **Dry-roast the besan first**, in the dry wok, until it smells nutty. Set aside and let it cool.
   > It binds by **protein coagulation**, which is far more forgiving than gluten, and roasted it also tastes of something. **It is the only ingredient here doing structure and flavour at once**, which is why it is in the list rather than more gluten.
3. 🍄 **Separate the stems from the caps.** **Shred the stems raw** — a fork dragged down the cut face, along the grain, working around the stem until it is all strands. **Slice the caps.** Then dry-cook the lot together: water released, water evaporated, browning. 🥑 **10 g of oil goes in only once the surfaces have gone matte.**
   > ⚠⚠ **The stem is the pulling material, and it has to be shredded raw.** A king oyster stem is densely packed parallel hyphae, and that alignment is the grain a fork follows. **Cook it first and it softens and smears instead of separating** — the structure that makes the strands possible is the thing heat destroys.
   > ⚠ **The caps have no grain.** They will not pull, so they are sliced and go in as a second texture. Nothing is wasted and nothing is forced. Full technique, and the table of which mushrooms pull and which do not: [`../../docs/TECHNIQUES.md`](../../docs/TECHNIQUES.md) → *King oyster — pulling*.
   > ⚠ **Not dice, under any circumstances.** Strands give fibre and irregularity, which is what reads as texture in the finished patty. Dice gives recognisable mushroom pieces, which is the exact thing this design is built to avoid.
4. 🧅 🧄 **Onion and garlic into the same wok, browned**, picking up whatever the mushrooms left behind.

### 3. Mix

5. **Blend a third of the cooled buckwheat to a paste**, then fold the whole grains back into it.
   > ✅ **This is where most of the binding comes from.** Buckwheat is high in amylose and sets as *discrete grains* — it holds its own shape and not its neighbours'. Blending a fraction releases the starch that makes the rest cohere. Same pattern as the corn soup's 400 charred / 700 blended / 400 whole.
6. **Fold in** the 🍄 mushrooms, 🧅 onion, 🫘 besan, 🌽 corn starch and 🍶 soy sauce. **Then the 💪 gluten last, and stop as soon as it is distributed.**
   > ⚠ 🍶 The soy goes in here rather than into the wok because the wok stage is about removing liquid, not adding it.
   > ⚠ 🌽 **The corn starch does nothing for raw handling** — it binds only once heated. It is there for the crust and the set, not to get the patties into the pan intact.
7. **Form the patties and chill them.** Twelve of roughly 90 g.

### 4. Fry

8. 🥑 **10 g of oil in a hot pan. One flip.** Leave them undisturbed until a crust forms — that crust is what releases them, and moving them early tears the face off.

## Design notes

- ⚠⚠ **The mushroom changed species after the design was written, and the reason is structural.** The brief said portobello. **Portobello has no grain** — its flesh is undifferentiated, so tearing it yields irregular crumb rather than strands, and crumb has nothing to interlock with. Holger had already tried pulling portobello and found it *"kind of worked but they did not hold up in a soup"*, which is the same failure in a wetter test. **A king oyster stem is densely packed parallel hyphae**, and that alignment is a grain in the woodworking sense: shredded raw along it, the stem separates into strands that are themselves load-bearing in the mix. ✅ **It is also the drier mushroom** — 88.1% water against 92.8% — which helps a format whose main enemy is free water. ⚠ **The cost is micronutrient, not structural**: see the Nutrition section, where selenium falls by two-thirds. **The gluten share of the mix drops from 5.7% to about 5.0%** because there is more cooked mushroom to dilute it, and that is acceptable precisely because the strands do structural work that portobello crumb did not.
- **Three binders, each doing a different job, and none of them is egg.** 🌾 **Blended buckwheat** supplies the bulk cohesion, 🫘 **besan** sets by protein coagulation on heating, 💪 **gluten** gives the elastic network that stops it crumbling. Corn starch is a fourth but only for the crust. **The reason for three rather than one is that each fails differently** — gluten alone goes bouncy, starch alone goes pasty, grain alone falls apart.
- ⚠ **Millet was considered and rejected, and the reasoning is worth keeping.** Glutinous millet is a *better* binder than buckwheat — near-pure amylopectin, cohesive without help — and there was 1075 g of it already cooked. It was dropped because **it carries almost no flavour**: mild and faintly sweet, where kasha is nutty and toasty. Swapping would have traded the dish's second flavour source for binding and left mushroom and soy doing everything. **Blending a fraction of the buckwheat gets the cohesion from the grain that already tastes of something.** ⚠ If the leftover millet is still around, a small proportion folded in would add cohesion at little flavour cost — but do not make it the base.
- **Why the onion is in.** Nothing else in the list supplies sweetness or allium, and mushroom-plus-soy is thin for a whole patty. It is the project's standard depth lever.
- ✅ **This is the format most likely to work for Lara, and that is deliberate.** Observation 58 holds that her mushroom problem is **form, not flavour** — she complained about 8 mm dice in a visible layer on the Zwiebelkuchen and scores blended and powdered mushroom near her ceiling. **Torn fine and bound into a patty, mushroom is texture rather than identifiable pieces.** She has never scored the Zwiebelkuchen at all. If she scores these, that settles observation 58 from the other direction.
