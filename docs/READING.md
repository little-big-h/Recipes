## Technique digest — Julie Sahni, *Classic Indian Cooking*

*Our own-words summary of the methods the book is known for teaching, for quick reference. Attributed to Sahni's approach; not a reproduction of her text or recipes. Cross-references our own `TECHNIQUES.md` where relevant.*

- **Browning onions is the foundation, and the colour is a dial.** Sahni treats the degree to which onions are cooked — soft and pale, golden, or deep brown — as the single biggest determinant of a North-Indian gravy's character: the darker the onion, the deeper and sweeter-savoury the base. The lesson is patience and reading the colour, not rushing to the next step. (See our `TECHNIQUES.md` → Bhuna.)
- **Bhuna / the sauté-braise of the masala.** The aromatic base (onion, ginger, garlic, then ground spices, then tomato) is fried in fat over patient heat until it browns and the **fat separates** — the visual cue that the base is "cooked," not raw. This concentrated, browned masala is the body of the finished dish, built before any main liquid or vegetable goes in.
- **Whole spices, dry-roasted then ground fresh.** A recurring emphasis: toasting whole spices in a dry pan to bloom their aroma, then grinding them just before use, rather than relying on pre-ground jars. Freshly made *garam masala* as a finishing warmth.
- **Tempering (tadka / baghar).** Blooming whole spices (and sometimes aromatics) in hot fat to release fat-soluble aromatics — used either at the *start* of a dish as the flavour foundation, or poured over at the *end* as a finishing burst. The same spice in raw vs. tempered form tastes entirely different.
- **Ginger-garlic, fried off.** Pounded or pureed ginger and garlic are fried until their raw, sharp smell mellows before the dish proceeds — skipping this leaves a harsh note.
- **Yogurt without splitting.** When yogurt enriches a sauce, it's whisked smooth and added gradually off a hard boil (and/or stabilised) so it doesn't curdle — gentle heat, patience again.
- **Sequence is the recipe.** Much of the method is *order*: aromatics → ground spices (brief, they scorch) → tomato/liquid to deglaze → simmer → finishing temper or garam masala. Getting the order and the browning right matters more than any single ingredient.
- **Rice as technique.** Rinsing to remove surface starch, soaking, and the absorption/pilaf method (toast the grains in fat, add measured liquid, steam undisturbed) for separate, fluffy grains.

**The through-line:** heat control and patience. Most of the depth in this style of cooking comes from *how long and how hot* you brown the base — exactly the bhuna lesson the project keeps relearning.

*To go deeper than this map, read the book — the value is in her step-by-step exposition, which we deliberately don't reproduce here.*
